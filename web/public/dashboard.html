Prop Desk — Strategy Review

Bitfunded Expert $100k · 1-Step · BTCUSDT 4h · cross-reference as of 15 Sep 2026

Smartest option

Five-tranche strength ladder + regime filter + desk cap — but do not buy a challenge yet. Run the walk-forward first.

It's the best structure found: the lowest drawdown of anything tested (−$1,805 against −$6,943 for single entry), zero zombie accounts, and a median challenge run ending at $99,060 instead of bleeding to the floor. It is also still −0.00R. That is a well-built vehicle with no engine in it.

Why wait: a $999 challenge buys one noisy binary outcome. The walk-forward buys ~5 years of out-of-sample evidence for the price of one Claude Code session. At a 14% industry base rate, passing once barely updates whether you have an edge — plenty of edgeless traders pass.

Cross-reference — things that don't add up

1. "5x scale-in for a larger position" does the opposite

design assumption inverted

At the same $500 risk, a 5-tranche strength ladder holds 34% less quantity than a single entry (0.109 BTC vs 0.167 BTC; $8,524 vs $13,000 notional). Because the stop stays anchored at T1's structure, each later tranche sits further from the stop and therefore gets less size.

The ladder's benefit is conditionality, not size: losers fill 1.63 tranches, winners fill 4.17. You're small when wrong and large when right. If what you actually want is a bigger position, that comes from raising risk per trade, and the ruin math says don't.

2. The income model assumed 4.3× the trade frequency

changes the conclusion

income_math.py ran at 30 trades/month. The strategy produces 7.1/month (53 trades in 7.5 months). Every encouraging number in the $5k/month analysis — the 4-months-to-target row, $614 at the real 9/mo frequency (was $1,862 at an assumed 30/mo)/account — assumed a frequency this strategy does not reach.

Corrected: at 7.1 trades/month and 0.5% risk, $6,250/month gross needs +1.77R per trade. Not credible at any skill level. Either the timeframe drops below 4h to raise frequency, or the income target needs more accounts than the scaling table implied.

3. RESULTS.md is stale on every row

documentation

All four published configs are off after the entry-bar reorder: A n=90→89 (−0.10→−0.11R), B 71→70 (−0.00→−0.01R), C 72→71 (−0.01→−0.03R), D 39→38 (−0.21→−0.18R). Small deltas, but the file also predates the merged reset block, the day-start basis, the zombie outcome, ladder direction, LADDER_N and the reverse.

4. The skill still describes the rollover trap as a general hazard

skill text

bitfunded.md presents it as broadly dangerous. Measured: it needs both uncapped risk ≥4% per position and a policy that holds losers through the reset. Under desk sizing plus swing_safe it is dead twice over — by cap and by policy. Largest floating loss actually carried into a reset was $3,422 against the $4,000 line, at 4% naive risk.

5. Your reverse-after-TP3 is untested, not refuted

insufficient n

It fired 4 times in 8 months, because only ~8% of trades reach TP3 at 3R. n=4 is noise. To evaluate the exhaustion thesis the trigger has to move to TP2 or the stop-out.

6. None of it is in the repo

unshipped

The reorder, merged reset block, day-start basis, zombie outcome, ladder direction, 5-tranche support, reverse, income_math.py and scaling_math.py exist only in a sandbox scratch copy. kunjancollective/prop-desk has none of it.

7. Two known small defects, both flagged and unfixed

minor

income_math.py's withdraw=False arm reports $0 by construction — it counts only withdrawals as income, so the "bank profits to grow the buffer" comparison can't be sized. And risk.py accepts --json only before the subcommand.

The budget cap is not conservatism — it is the only thing that makes the account survivable. At +0.35R, a genuinely excellent system, naive sizing at 1% blows the account 68% of the time within a year, and at 2% it's 98%. Every one of those cells is 0.0% under the cap.

What would change the verdict

  1. Walk-forward comes back >+0.10R out-of-sample with a stable parameter neighbourhood — fit 2021–2024, hold out 2025–now. This is the gate on everything else.
  2. Frequency reaches ~30 trades/month, which on this structure means dropping below the 4h timeframe. Without it the income target needs roughly four times the accounts the scaling model suggested.
  3. Reverse moves to TP2 or stop-out so the exhaustion thesis gets a real sample instead of n=4.
  4. Bitfunded's scaling plan — 10% net for 4 consecutive months, 2 withdrawals — grows one account instead of buying four. Cheaper, less to manage, and four correlated accounts running one strategy aren't four independent bets anyway.
