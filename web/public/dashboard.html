Hypothetical performance

These results are based on simulated or hypothetical performance results that have certain inherent limitations. Unlike the results shown in an actual performance record, these results do not represent actual trading. Also, because these trades have not actually been executed, these results may have under-or over-compensated for the impact, if any, of certain market factors, such as lack of liquidity. Simulated or hypothetical trading programs in general are also subject to the fact that they are designed with the benefit of hindsight. No representation is being made that any account will or is likely to achieve profits or losses similar to these being shown.

troid's own strategy shows no statistical edge. Out of sample, on 504 BTC trades from 1 January 2021 to 7 January 2026, which the parameters never saw, it measures +0.008R per trade, standard error 0.016R — a MEASURED figure (backtest/WALKFORWARD.md), inside noise, and not a fact about the future.

Lowest-drawdown structure tested

Five-tranche strength ladder + regime filter + desk cap — but do not buy a challenge. The walk-forward has been run, and it came back as noise.

Of everything tested it has the lowest drawdown (−$1,058 against −$7,295 for single entry), and across 72 rolling challenge starts none failed, none stalled and none passed: every one was still running at the end of the sample, median balance $100,409. It is +0.03R a trade on the sample it was chosen on, and +0.008R on the 504 BTC trades from 1 January 2021 to 7 January 2026 it never saw. That is a well-built vehicle with no engine in it.

Why not: a $999 challenge buys one noisy binary outcome. Five years of holdout just said the entry logic has no measurable edge. At the 14% pass rate in FPFX Tech's data (SOURCED · reported by Finance Magnates, published 2024-09-18, read 2026-09-26; Sources), passing once barely updates whether you have an edge — plenty of edgeless traders pass.

What the research found, including where troid was wrong

1. A 5-tranche ladder holds less, not more

measured on the sample

At the same $500 risk, a 5-tranche strength ladder holds 34% less quantity than a single entry (0.109 BTC against 0.167 BTC on a 1.5-ATR stop). Because the stop stays anchored at the first tranche's structure, each later tranche sits further from the stop and therefore gets less size.

The ladder's benefit is conditionality, not size: losers fill 1.66 tranches, winners fill 4.26. You're small when wrong and large when right. If what you actually want is a bigger position, that comes from raising risk per trade, and the ruin math says don't.

2. troid's income model assumed about three times the real trade frequency

changes the conclusion

troid's income model (income_math.py) assumed 30 trades a month. The strategy produces 10.1 a month (78 trades in 7.7 months), so each account earns about a third of what the model showed.

At 10.1 trades a month and 0.5% risk, $6,250 a month gross needs +1.24R per trade ($6,250 ÷ $500 ÷ 10.1), far above the +0.008R the strategy measured out of sample. Either the timeframe drops below 4h to trade more often, or the target needs more accounts.

3. The published statistics are generated, never written by hand

resolved

The results table was once written by hand, and it drifted from the engine after every change. It is now generated by gen_results.py from the engine on the frozen sample, and verify_claims.py re-derives the published statistics from journal.csv and fails if either drifts. The table below is that output.

4. The rollover trap needs two conditions at once

measured on the sample

Holding a position through the daily reset is often described as broadly dangerous. Measured over 72 rolling challenge starts for each of the 10 configurations, no position carried across a reset broke the $4,000 daily limit. The closest took uncapped 4% risk and a policy that holds losing positions through the reset together: a $3,448 floating loss against the $4,000 line. With troid's desk sizing the worst was $431; with its holding policy, which closes a losing position before the reset, none was carried.

5. The reversal trigger moved to the stop-out; still one regime

still one regime

Triggered on the third target, the reverse fired 5 times in 7.7 months, because only about 9% of trades reach it. Triggered on the stop-out, it fires 24 times in the 78-trade sample, which is a sample rather than an anecdote, but it is still one asset over one regime, and the holdout (1 January 2021 to 7 January 2026) says the whole configuration is inside noise.

6. Everything is public

resolved

Everything is in troid's public repository, kunjancollective/troid, and troid's ledger runs daily from .github/workflows/shadow.yml: fresh bars, replay, journal, ledger, commit. Each journal row is committed when it is logged, with its logged_utc, so the repository's history shows when every trade was recorded.

7. Two known defects in troid's tools, not yet fixed

minor

In income_math.py, the option that keeps profits in the account reports no income by construction, because it counts only withdrawals, so it can't be compared with withdrawing. And risk.py reads --json only before its subcommand.

The budget cap is not conservatism: it is what makes the account survivable. At +0.35R, a genuinely excellent system, naive sizing at 1% blows the account 68% of the time within a year, and at 2% it's 100%. Under the cap, every one of those cells is 0%.

What would change the verdict

  1. Walk-forward comes back >+0.10R out-of-sample with a stable parameter neighbourhood. This is the gate on everything else. Run 21 Sep 2026 on api.binance.us: the config was chosen on 2026; on the 504 BTC trades from 1 January 2021 to 7 January 2026 it never saw, +0.008R, 95% CI [−0.023R, +0.040R]. ETH, 498 trades: +0.008R. Not met (MEASURED: WALKFORWARD.md).
  2. Frequency reaches about 30 trades a month, the rate the income model assumed, which on this structure means dropping below the 4h timeframe.
  3. Reverse moves to TP2 or stop-out so the exhaustion thesis gets a real sample instead of 5 trades. Done: reverse-on-stop is the config, 24 reverse trades in the sample (RESULTS.md). It did not change the verdict.

What troid is testing next

troid's own strategy has been tested on five years of data it never saw, and measures inside noise: no measurable edge. Next:

  1. A second strategy, troid-shadow-2, built separately so the first stays as it was: momentum over months, position size set by volatility, and the same loss limits and firm rules.
  2. Both strategies across at least 10 assets, not only BTC. A rule that works on BTC alone was fitted to BTC.
  3. The same walk-forward test for both, reported side by side. If neither survives it, that is the finding, and troid says so.
  4. Whether troid ever trades a challenge with its own strategy waits on that result.
