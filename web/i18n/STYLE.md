# How troid sounds in every language

For whoever drafts or reviews a translation. troid never hypes; that is the brand (BRAND.md). The same holds in
every language.

## Voice

- Flat, precise, slightly dry. Lead with the number. Never excited, never reassuring, never selling.
- No exclamation marks, in any script (! ！ ¡).
- troid speaks of itself in the third person where the language allows: "troid computes", not "we compute".
  Never "I", "we" or "our" for troid. Where a language makes the third person stiff, prefer an impersonal
  construction to the first person.
- Address the reader the way a careful, modern product in that language does (see the table), and keep the same
  form throughout.

## Names

- **troid** stays exactly `troid`: lowercase, Latin letters, never transliterated, never capitalised, even at the
  start of a sentence.
- troid's products have one fixed translation per language, recorded once under `product.*` and used identically
  everywhere else: troid's desk, troid's compare, troid's ledger, troid's research, ask troid. Keep `troid`
  inside each.
- Firm names (Bitfunded, BrightFunded, Crypto Fund Trader), product labels (1-Step, 2-Phase, Instant), tickers
  (BTC, BTCUSDT), units (R, ×, %), UTC and file names stay as they are.

## Figures and code

- Every number stays exactly as in the English, in Latin digits (0-9) — never Arabic-Indic, Devanagari or Bengali
  digits. The site formats numbers for the reader's locale by itself where it computes them.
- Placeholders such as `{n}`, `{firm}`, `{L}`, `{H}` stay exactly as written; the site fills them. Move them to
  wherever the sentence needs them.
- HTML tags and link targets stay exactly as they are; translate only the words between them.
- Formulas keep their structure and symbols; translate the words in them (equity, quota, day start).

## What stays in English

The terms of use, the 17 CFR 4.41 hypothetical-performance text, each firm's required sentence, and rule-source
names (such as "help centre: Criteria to be Success"). The site shows them in English with a one-line summary in
your language and "This translation is provided for convenience. The English version governs." — which you
translate.
