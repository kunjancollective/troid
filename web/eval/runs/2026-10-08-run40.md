## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **23.1 s**

> Two different ceilings, and which one binds on a given day depends on the day's starting balance, not equity alone.
> 
> Formula: daily budget = daily limit + (equity − day start), where the daily floor = day start − quota × daily%, and drawdown budget = equity − (quota × (1 − max%)). The binding ceiling = min(daily budget, drawdown budget).
> 
> **The daily limit** is a fixed amount measured from the account's initial balance (quota), reset each day. On Bitfunded's 1-Step (quota $100,000, daily 4%, max 6%, static drawdown), on a day that starts and sits at $100,000: daily floor = $100,000 − $100,000 × 4% = $96,000, daily budget = $100,000 − $96,000 = $4,000.
> 
> **The maximum loss** is a floor measured from the starting quota that never resets (static on Bitfunded): max-loss floor = $100,000 × (1 − 6%) = $94,000, drawdown budget = $100,000 − $94,000 = $6,000.
> 
> On that $100,000 day-start example, min($4,000, $6,000) = $4,000 — the daily limit binds.
> 
> Take a day that instead starts at $97,000 (equity also $97,000): daily floor = $97,000 − $100,000 × 4% = $93,000, daily budget = $97,000 − $93,000 = $4,000; max-loss floor is still $94,000, so drawdown budget = $97,000 − $94,000 = $3,000. Now min($4,000, $3,000) = $3,000 — the max-loss floor binds instead.
> 
> The crossover between the two is the day-start balance where they're equal: quota × (1 − max% + daily%) = $100,000 × (1 − 0.06 + 0.04) = $98,000. Below $98,000 at the day's start, the floor binds; from $98,000 up to and above the $100,000 start, the daily limit binds — a day starting less than $2,000 below the start is still bound by the daily limit.
> 
> What it means: the daily limit isn't a flat $4,000 every day in practice — once the day-start balance has drifted down near $98,000, the real constraint becomes the $94,000 floor, which is tighter than the advertised daily percentage suggests. Both ceilings count floating, open-position losses on Bitfunded, not just realized ones — reaching either one fails the account with no close required.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **20.2 s**

> The stop sets the position size — without it troid can't compute how many units turn a given dollar risk into the loss that occurs if the stop is hit.
> 
> Formula: qty = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee_unit), where fee_unit = fee per side × (entry + stop) — the exit fee is charged at the stop.
>
