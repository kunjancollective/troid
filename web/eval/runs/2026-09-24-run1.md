**Tools:** none · **model:** claude-haiku-4-5 · **7.5 s**

> The two ceilings work on different timescales and bind in different circumstances.
> 
> **Daily loss limit:** a fixed amount, reset every day. On Bitfunded's 1-Step it is 4% of the initial account balance — so $4,000 on a $100k account. It sets the ceiling for losses in the current calendar day. Every 24 hours (at Bitfunded, 16:00–16:10 UTC), the clock resets and a fresh $4,000 budget arrives, regardless of whether the account is up or down. A floating loss survives the reset and counts at full size against the new day's budget.
> 
> **Maximum loss:** a hard floor on the account's equity, measured over the life of the challenge or funded stage. On Bitfunded's 1-Step that floor is 6% below the starting balance — so equity cannot fall below $94,000 on a $100k account. This is a static floor: it never moves. Once breached, the account is disqualified.
> 
> **Why there are two:** the daily limit controls short-term variance — a trader cannot blow half the account in one bad day. The maximum loss controls long-term ruin — it sets a point where the account cannot recover and the challenge is over. They bind at different equity levels.
> 
> **Crossover.** On a fresh day the two swap control at `equity = quota × (1 − max% + daily%)`. For the 1-Step:
> 
> ```
> $100,000 × (1 − 0.06 + 0.04) = $100,000 × 0.98 = $98,000
> ```
> 
> Above $98,000 the daily limit binds — you can only lose $4,000 today. Below it the maximum loss binds — you cannot lose more than $6,000 total. The crossover sits just $2,000 into a bad day, so below it, one more loss at the daily budget would breach the max. That is why sizing against the binding ceiling matters: which one is smaller depends on where equity sits right now.

> 
> **Formula:** `risk = |entry − stop| × quantity`
>

## q-expectancy — quantitative

**Question:** My win rate is 40% and my average win is 1.5R against an average loss of 1R. What's my expectancy, and what win rate would I need to break even?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **12.5 s**

> Expectancy = 0R, and the break-even win rate is exactly 40% — the win rate given.
> 
> **Formula:** E = p × W − (1 − p) × L; break-even win rate = L ÷ (W + L) = 1 ÷ (1 + W/L)
> 
> **Why it works:** expectancy is the average result per trade, in R, over many repetitions. It weighs each outcome (win or loss) by how often it happens. The break-even win rate is the p at which those two terms cancel — the payoff ratio (W/L) alone sets it.
> 
> **Worked example (your numbers):**
> - p = 0.40
> - E = 0.4 × 1.5 − 0.6 × 1 = 0.60 − 0.60 = **0R**
> - payoff ratio W/L = 1.5
> - break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + 1.5) = **40%**
> 
> **What it means:** at a 40% win rate with a 1.5R average win against a 1R average loss, the system is sitting exactly at its break-even point — long-run expectancy is zero, not positive or negative. Any edge above zero requires either a higher win rate than 40% at this same payoff ratio, or a larger payoff ratio at this same win rate. This is arithmetic on the numbers given (DERIVED), not a comment on any specific trade or firm rule.
