**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.9 s**

> No — the loss at the stop is the same at 10× as at 2×, as long as the margin needed fits inside the account's equity. Leverage changes the margin posted and where exchange liquidation sits, not the size of the loss.
> 
> Formula:
> quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee per unit)
> notional = quantity × entry
> margin = notional ÷ leverage
> loss at the stop = quantity × (|entry − stop| + fee per unit) = risk
> 
> Leverage appears only in the margin line, not in quantity or the loss — unless the margin that size would need is more than the equity, in which case the size is cut to quantity = equity × leverage ÷ entry, and then a lower leverage does mean a smaller size and a smaller loss.
> 
> Worked example, on a $100,000 account, entry 60,000, stop 59,000 (stop distance 1,000), risking $1,000:
> 
> At 2× leverage: quantity = 1,000 ÷ (1,000 + 0) = 1. notional = 1 × 60,000 = $60,000. margin = 60,000 ÷ 2 = $30,000. loss at the stop = $1,000.
> 
> At 10× leverage: quantity = 1,000 ÷ (1,000 + 0) = 1. notional = 1 × 60,000 = $60,000. margin = 60,000 ÷ 10 = $6,000. loss at the stop = $1,000.
> 
> Same quantity, same loss ($1,000) at both leverages — only the margin posted differs ($30,000 vs $6,000). In both cases the margin fits inside the $100,000 equity, so nothing gets cut.
> 
> What this means for your position: using 10× instead of 2× here ties up less margin and moves exchange liquidation closer to entry, but it does not make the stop-out loss larger. The loss is set by the stop distance and the position size, not by leverage — it only changes if the margin at that size would exceed equity, forcing a smaller size.
