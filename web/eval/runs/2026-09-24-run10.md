## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **14.7 s**

> Two different ceilings, tracked separately, and only the tighter one matters on any given day.
> 
> **The daily loss limit** resets every day and is a fixed dollar amount taken from the initial balance (Bitfunded FAQ): `daily_floor = day_start − quota × daily%`. It's a floor for today only — lose that much from wherever the balance started today, and the account fails, regardless of how much room remains overall.
> 
> **The maximum loss** is a floor for the whole account, measured from the quota once (on a static-drawdown product): `dd_floor = quota × (1 − max%)`. It doesn't reset; every day's losses eat into the same floor cumulatively.
> 
> Worked on troid's reference account — a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step, quota $100,000, equity $100,000, day-start $100,000, 4% daily / 6% max (help centre — Challenge & Trader Stage; Terms of Use 9(a), read 2026-09-23):
> 
> - daily floor = 100,000 − 100,000×4% = **$96,000** → daily budget = equity − daily floor = **$4,000**
> - max-loss floor = 100,000×(1−6%) = **$94,000** → drawdown budget = equity − max-loss floor = **$6,000**
> - binding = min(4,000, 6,000) = **the daily loss limit, $4,000**
> 
> At the start of a fresh day, the daily limit is almost always the tighter one, because it resets small each day while the max-loss floor only gets closer as losses accumulate over multiple days. The two swap at the crossover equity, `quota × (1 − max% + daily%) = $98,000` here: above it, the daily limit binds; below it, the wider-looking 6% headline is already spent and the max-loss floor is what actually stops the trader. Both budgets count floating losses on open positions, not just closed ones — Bitfunded auto-fails on either without requiring a close.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **17.4 s**

> The stop price sets the loss per unit, which is what turns a risk amount in dollars into a position size — leverage and account balance alone can't do that.
> 
> Formula: `qty = risk / (|entry − stop| + fee_unit)`, where risk is the dollar amount being risked on the trade and fee_unit is the round-trip fee cost per unit (entry × fee per side × 2).

## q-stats — quantitative

**Question:** Over 78 trades my average is +0.033R with a standard deviation of 0.40R. Is that a real edge?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.3 s**

> 
> Formula: standard error = sd ÷ √n; t = mean ÷ SE; 95% confidence interval = mean ± 1.96 × SE.
> 
> Why it works: a mean computed from a noisy, finite sample is itself uncertain. The standard error measures that uncertainty, and the confidence interval shows the range of true means consistent with what was observed. If zero falls inside it, the data can't rule out "no edge at all."
> 
> Worked example, with the numbers given: mean = 0.033R, sd = 0.40R, n = 78.
> - SE = 0.40 ÷ √78 = 0.0453R
> - t = 0.033 ÷ 0.0453 = 0.729
> - 95% CI = 0.033 ± 1.96 × 0.0453 = [−0.0558R, 0.1218R]
>
