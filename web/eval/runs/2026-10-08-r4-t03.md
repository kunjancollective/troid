**Tools:** check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **16.5 s**

> Each one caps a different thing, and which one binds depends on where the day starts.
> 
> The daily limit resets every day and measures loss against a fixed point: on Bitfunded's 1-Step it's a fixed amount from the account's initial balance, not from today's balance. The maximum loss is a static floor set once, from the account's starting quota, and never resets — it's the total drawdown allowed over the life of the account.
> 
> Formulas, as troid's desk uses them:
> 
> daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)
> dd_budget = equity − dd_floor, where dd_floor = quota × (1 − max_loss%)
> 
> Working this on troid's reference account, a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step: the daily floor sits at $96,000 (day start $100,000 − 4% of quota = $4,000) and the max-loss floor at $94,000 (quota × (1 − 6%)). That gives a daily budget of $4,000 and a drawdown budget of $6,000 — the daily limit is the smaller one, so it binds here.
> 
> Which one binds turns on the day's start alone: below $98,000 at the day's start the max-loss floor binds and the advertised daily limit is fiction; between $98,000 and the $100,000 start, and above the start, the daily limit binds. $98,000 is the crossover (quota × (1 − max% + daily%) = $100,000 × (1 − 6% + 4%)) — the day-start balance at which the two budgets are equal. Which one binds depends on the day-start balance, not on equity alone.
> 
> Both floors count floating losses on open positions, not just closed ones, and Bitfunded auto-fails the account the moment either is reached, no close required.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** size_trade, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **24.8 s**

> The stop sets the position size — without it troid can't know how many units of risk you're taking.
> 
> Formula: quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee_unit), where risk is the dollar amount you're willing to lose and fee_unit is the per-unit entry-plus-exit fee cost, (entry + stop) × fee per side. The loss at the stop, quantity × (|entry − stop| + fee_unit), equals the risk you set — that's the whole point of a stop: it fixes how much a trade can cost before anything else does.
> 
> Here's a worked example on troid's reference account, a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step, entry 77,872, long, risking 0.5% of equity:
> 
> With a stop at 76,580 (distance 1,292), the fee per unit is (77,872 + 76,580) × 0.04% = 61.7808, so quantity = 500 ÷ (1,292 + 61.7808) = 0.369336. That sets the notional at 0.369336 × 77,872 = $28,760.93 and the margin at 5× leverage at $5,752.19, with fees of $22.82.
> 
> With a stop at 77,372 instead (distance 500), the fee per unit is (77,872 + 77,372) × 0.04% = 62.0976, so quantity = 500 ÷ (500 + 62.0976) = 0.889525. That sets the notional at $69,269.11 and the margin at 5× leverage at $13,853.82, with fees of $55.24. The loss at the stop is $500 either way — the risk you chose — but the quantity, notional, margin and fees all follow from where the stop sits.
> 
> What this means for you: the stop isn't an extra input alongside the risk — it's the denominator that turns a dollar risk into a position. Without it troid has no distance to divide the risk by, and therefore no size, no notional, no margin, and no fee figure to report.
