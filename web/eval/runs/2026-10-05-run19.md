> 
> **Formula:** 1R = |entry − stop| × position size
>

>

## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, firm_rules · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **18.3 s**

> Every funded account has two separate loss ceilings, and they're not the same thing.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** — how much equity can fall from the start of the current trading day before the account fails. On Bitfunded's 1-Step this is 4%, and it's a fixed amount measured from the *initial* account balance, not the day's balance — the floor it sets is measured from the day's start, so today's profit or loss offsets today's remaining room (*Bitfunded FAQ*; Challenge & Trader Stage, One Step Evaluation table; Terms of Use 9(a)).
> 
> **Maximum loss** — how much equity can fall from a reference point over the life of the account, not just one day. On Bitfunded's 1-Step it is 6%, and it's static: measured from the account's starting quota, so a trader who is up money has permanently widened this buffer (help centre, *Challenge & Trader Stage* and *Criteria to be Success*; Terms of Use 9(a)). Other products work differently — some trail a high-water mark instead of staying static — so a drawdown type belongs to a specific product, not a whole firm.
> 
> Both of Bitfunded's ceilings count **floating** losses on open positions, not only closed ones: an open position that reaches either limit fails the account immediately, with no close required (help centre, *Criteria to be Success*).
> 
> What this means practically: the two ceilings bind at different equity levels, and whichever is smaller at any moment is the one that actually constrains the next trade — troid calls this the binding budget, and it can be checked for a specific account given its quota and equity.

## p-crossover — prop-rule

> 
> **Formula:** crossover = quota × (1 − max% + daily%)
>

> $100,000 × (1 − 0.06 + 0.04) = $100,000 × 0.98 = **$98,000**
>
