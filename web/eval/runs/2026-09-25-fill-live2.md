## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **16.8 s**

> The stop is the input that fixes how much a given position size actually risks.
> 
> **Formula:** `quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee_unit)`, where risk is the dollar amount being risked (e.g. a percent of equity), and fee_unit is the round-trip fee added to the risk per unit.
> 
> **Why it works:** risk in dollars is not a property of the position size alone — it's the size multiplied by how far price has to move against the trade before it's cut. Without a stop distance, "how much am I risking" has no answer; the same position size can risk $50 or $5,000 depending only on where the stop sits. Leverage doesn't substitute for it either: leverage sets margin and liquidation distance, not the loss at the stop.
> 
> **In practice, worked through the tools:**
> 
> With entry 77,872 and stop 76,580, a $500 risk gives a stop distance of 1,292 plus a fee of about 62.30 per unit (Bitfunded's 0.04% per side, help centre — Criteria to be Success), so quantity comes out to about 0.3692 units, notional about $28,750, margin about $5,750 at 5× leverage.
> 
> **What it means:** without the stop, none of that resolves — quantity, notional, margin and fee cost all fall out of the stop distance. Changing the stop to, say, half that distance would roughly double the quantity for the same $500 risked. That's also why troid never invents a stop for you: the stop is the trader's decision, and it's the one number the rest of the sizing depends on.
