**Question:** Should I size with Kelly? I win 45% of my trades and my average win is twice my average loss.

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **12.9 s**

> troid doesn't recommend; it prices what you bring. Here's what the Kelly formula implies for the numbers given.
> 
> **Full Kelly comes out to 17.5% of the account risked per trade.**
> 
> Formula: `f* = p − (1 − p) ÷ b`, where p is win rate and b is average win ÷ average loss.
> 
> Why it works: Kelly picks the risk fraction that maximizes long-run compounded growth — but only if p and b are exactly right, which in live trading they never are, since they're estimated from a finite, noisy sample of past trades.
> 
> Worked example, your numbers: p = 0.45, b = 2 (average win twice average loss).
> `f* = 0.45 − 0.55 ÷ 2 = 0.45 − 0.275 = 0.175` → 17.5%. Half-Kelly, the fraction often used to reduce sensitivity to estimation error, is 8.75%.
> 
> What this means for a Bitfunded 1-Step account: the maximum loss is 6% of the starting balance and the daily loss limit is 4% (help centre, Challenge & Trader Stage, and Terms of Use 9(a), read 2026-09-23). The daily limit is smaller still, so full Kelly is 4.38× the daily allowance. On this account structure, the firm's ceiling binds long before Kelly would, which is why troid's sizing works against the remaining budget rather than against a Kelly fraction.

## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **14.8 s**

> The daily loss limit resets every day; the maximum loss (drawdown) does not — it applies to the whole account for the life of the challenge.
> 
> Formula for each, on Bitfunded's 1-Step (troid's reference account, $100,000):
> 
> ```
> daily_floor = day_start_balance − quota × daily%
> daily_budget = equity − daily_floor
> 
> dd_floor (static) = quota × (1 − max%)
> dd_budget = equity − dd_floor
> ```
> 
> Why the distinction matters: the daily limit is measured against a fixed amount from the **initial balance**, reset each trading day (Bitfunded FAQ). The maximum loss is a static floor set once, from the account's starting quota, and never resets — it accumulates every loss across the whole account's life (Bitfunded help centre — Criteria to be Success). Both count **floating** losses on open positions, not just realized ones: an open position that reaches either limit fails the account with no close required (same source, read 2026-09-24).
> 
> Worked example, on the $100,000 1-Step at day start = equity = $100,000:
> 
> - daily floor = $100,000 − 100,000 × 4% = **$96,000**
> - daily budget = $100,000 − $96,000 = **$4,000**
> - max-loss floor (static) = $100,000 × (1 − 6%) = **$94,000**
> - drawdown budget = $100,000 − $94,000 = **$6,000**
> - binding ceiling here = min($4,000, $6,000) = **$4,000, the daily limit**
> 
> What it means for this account: at the start of a fresh day with no prior profit, the daily limit is the tighter constraint — $4,000 of room before either ceiling trips, not $6,000. The two swap which one binds at a day-start equity of $98,000 (the crossover): above it, the daily limit governs; below it, the maximum loss does, and the smaller daily number stops mattering because it's already been eaten into. A trader watching only the advertised "4% daily" figure below that crossover point is watching the wrong number.

## b-stop — beginner

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **20.2 s**

> The stop price sets the size of the loss — without it, no position size can be computed that keeps the risk at a chosen dollar or percent amount.
