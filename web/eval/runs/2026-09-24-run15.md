## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, explain_rule · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **21.7 s**

> They're two separate ceilings on an account, and they're reset differently in time.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** — a floor that resets each trading day. On Bitfunded's 1-Step it's a fixed 4% of the account's initial balance (never the day's current balance), and today's net P&L moves how much room is left under it:
> 
> `daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)`
> 
> **Maximum loss** — a floor for the life of the account, not reset daily. On Bitfunded's 1-Step it's static: measured from the account's starting quota, so `dd_floor = quota × (1 − 6%)`, and profit permanently widens the room under it (it never rises against you). Other firms' products can instead be trailing — the floor follows a high-water mark up as the account gains, tightening the room again — e.g. BrightFunded's 1-Step trails on equity, Crypto Fund Trader's 1-Phase trails on balance, both locking static once the account is 6% up; Crypto Fund Trader's 2-Phase is static from the start like Bitfunded's.
> 
> Both ceilings count **floating** losses, not just closed ones — on Bitfunded, an open position that reaches either one fails the account with no close required.
> 
> The two ceilings swap which one binds as the day's starting balance moves: they're equal where `day_start = quota × (1 − max% + daily%)`. On a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step that's $98,000 — only $2,000 below the start. A day that starts below $98,000 is bound by the max-loss floor, not the advertised 4% daily limit; above it, the daily limit binds. Which ceiling binds depends on that day's starting balance, not on equity alone.
> 
> Sources, each with the date troid read it:
