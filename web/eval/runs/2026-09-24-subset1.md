## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **23.1 s**

> The stop price fixes how much a position-size calculation lets ride on the trade.
> 
> **Formula:** quantity equals the risk divided by the distance between entry and stop, plus a fee amount per unit. Risk is a dollar amount the trader is willing to lose — a percent of equity, capped against the account's loss budget — and the entry-to-stop distance is the per-unit gap between them.
> 
> **Why it works:** the stop is what turns a dollar risk figure into a position size. Without it, "risk $500" is meaningless — $500 spread over a 1% stop buys a very different quantity than $500 spread over a 0.1% stop. The stop distance is the denominator; get it wrong and the position size, the fee drag, and the number of losses left before a loss ceiling trips are all wrong with it.
> 
> **Worked example**, computed for entry 77,872 and stop 76,580 on a Bitfunded 1-Step, risking $500:
> 
> - Stop distance: |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292
> - Fee per unit: 77,872 × 0.04% × 2 = 62.30 (Bitfunded's per-side fee, from the help centre, Criteria to be Success, read 2026-09-18 and 2026-09-23)
> - Quantity: 500 ÷ (1,292 + 62.30) = 0.369195
> - Notional: 0.369195 × 77,872 = $28,749.96
> - Margin at 5× leverage: $28,749.96 ÷ 5 = $5,749.99
> 
> Leverage sets the margin posted, not the quantity — the loss at the stop is the same at any leverage.
> 
> **What it means for you:** move the stop and every one of those numbers changes — the quantity, the notional, the margin, and how much of the risk goes to fees. The stop is the only input that fixes what "$500 of risk" actually means in position size, whether it's given as a level or as a percent distance from entry. The risk, the position, the stop and the trade are the trader's; troid prices them.

## b-leverage — beginner

**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **16.5 s**

> No — leverage does not change the loss at the stop. The loss is fixed by your risk (entry to stop distance × position size); leverage only changes the margin posted and where exchange liquidation sits, not the P&L when the stop fills.
> 
> Formula: risk = |entry − stop| × quantity — leverage doesn't appear in it.
> 
> Why it works: leverage is a financing choice about how much of the position's notional you post as margin. It moves the liquidation price closer or farther from entry, but the position's size and the distance to your stop are what determine the dollar loss.
> 
> Worked example, so the numbers aren't abstract: take a long entry at 100,000 with a stop at 99,000 (a 1% stop) and a quantity of 1 unit.
> 
> At 2× leverage: quantity = 1, notional = 100,000, margin posted = 50,000.
> At 10× leverage: quantity = 1, notional = 100,000, margin posted = 10,000.
> 
> The quantity and notional are identical in both cases, so the loss at the stop (1,000 × 1 = 1,000) is the same. Only the margin posted differs — 50,000 vs 10,000.
> 
> What it means for you: raising leverage from 2× to 10× frees up margin for other use and moves the exchange's liquidation price closer to your entry, but it does not change what you lose when your stop is hit — that's set by the stop distance and the quantity, not the leverage.
