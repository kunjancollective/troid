## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **16.9 s**

> They're two separate ceilings that can bind at different times, and an account can fail on either one first.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** — a fixed amount, reset each trading day, measuring loss from the balance at that day's start (or from the initial balance, depending on the firm's stated basis).
> `daily_floor = day_start − quota × daily%`
> `daily_budget = equity − daily_floor`
> 
> **Maximum loss** — the total drawdown allowed over the life of the account, from either a fixed point (static) or a trailing high (BrightFunded's model, following the high-water mark up but stopping at the quota).
> `dd_floor = quota × (1 − max%)` (static)
> `dd_budget = equity − dd_floor`
> 
> Whichever gives the smaller remaining room is the one that actually binds right now:
> `effective_budget = min(daily_budget, dd_budget)`
> 
> **Worked example** — Bitfunded's $100,000 1-Step, day-start equity $100,000:
> 
> - daily floor = 100,000 − 100,000×4% = 96,000 → daily budget = 100,000 − 96,000 = **$4,000**
> - max-loss floor = 100,000×(1−6%) = 94,000 → drawdown budget = 100,000 − 94,000 = **$6,000**
> - binding = min(4,000, 6,000) = **daily loss limit, $4,000**
> 
> At day-start equity of $100,000 the daily limit is tighter, so it's the one that actually constrains sizing. The two swap at a crossover equity of $98,000 (`quota × (1 − max% + daily%)`): below that starting balance, the maximum-loss floor becomes the binding one instead, and the advertised daily limit stops mattering. Both count floating losses on open positions, not just realized ones.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** size_trade · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **25.7 s**

> The stop is what turns a dollar risk into a position size — without it, there's no way to know how many units of the asset one dollar of risk buys.
> 
> **Formula:**
> `quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee per unit)`
> 
> **Why it works:** troid isn't sizing "how much money" — it's sizing "how much can be lost before this trade is wrong." That loss is |entry − stop| per unit. The dollar risk (say, 0.5% of equity) divided by that per-unit loss gives the only quantity that keeps the loss at the intended amount. Change the stop and every downstream number changes: quantity, notional, margin, and how many losses in a row exhaust the firm's loss ceiling.
> 
> **Worked example** (Bitfunded's reference account: $100,000 1-Step, long, entry 77,872):
> 
> - stop at 76,580 → stop distance = |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292
> - fee per unit = entry × 0.04% × 2 sides = 62.30 (Bitfunded's fee, help centre — Criteria to be Success, read 2026-09-18 and 2026-09-23)
> - risk = 0.5% × $100,000 = $500
> - quantity = 500 ÷ (1,292 + 62.30) = 0.3692
> - notional = 0.3692 × 77,872 = $28,750; margin at 5× = $5,750
> 
> If instead the stop sat at 77,000 (a 872 distance), the same $500 risk would buy a larger quantity and a larger notional — troid cannot know which without the stop.
> 
> **What it means:** for this trader, the stop 1,292 below entry is what fixes both the $500 risk and everything derived from it — quantity, margin, fee share (4.6% of risk here), and how many such losses (8, at this size) the daily loss limit of $4,000 (day-start $100,000 basis, Bitfunded FAQ, read 2026-09-21) can absorb before it trips. Without a stop, troid has a risk budget but no way to turn it into a tradable size.
