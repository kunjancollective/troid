> R is the amount risked on one trade — the loss taken if the stop is hit, used as a single unit to measure results.
> 
> Formula: `1R = |entry − stop| × position size`
> 
> Why it works: expressing every trade's outcome in multiples of R lets you compare trades of different sizes, symbols and prices on one common scale — a +2R winner is twice the size of the risk taken, regardless of the dollar amounts involved.
>

>

>

## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.5 s**

> The daily loss limit and the maximum loss are two separate floors under equity, and only the lower of the two governs at any moment.
> 
> **Formulas**
> 
> ```
> daily_floor  = day_start − quota × daily%
> daily_budget = equity − daily_floor
> 
> dd_floor     = quota × (1 − max%)          [static, Bitfunded]
>              = high_water_mark − quota × max%   [trailing, BrightFunded/CFT]
> dd_budget    = equity − dd_floor
> 
> binding      = min(daily_budget, dd_budget)
> ```
> 
> **Why they're different.** The daily limit resets every day (against the day-start balance for Bitfunded, per its FAQ), so it only measures today's damage. The maximum loss is cumulative over the whole account's life — it never resets. Which one actually stops you first depends on where equity sits relative to both floors; that crossing point is called the crossover.
> 
> **Worked example** — Bitfunded's $100,000 1-Step (troid's reference account), at day start = equity = $100,000:
> 
> - daily floor = 100,000 − 100,000×4% = **$96,000** → daily budget = $4,000
> - dd floor (static) = 100,000×(1−6%) = **$94,000** → dd budget = $6,000
> - binding = min(4,000, 6,000) = **daily loss limit, $4,000**
> 
> The crossover — the day-start equity at which the two swap — works out to $98,000 (`quota × (1 − max% + daily%)`). Above $98,000 the daily limit binds; below it, the max loss does, and the advertised daily-limit number stops being the real constraint.
> 
> **What it means:** on a fresh $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step, the daily limit is the tighter ceiling and governs sizing today; but after a losing day drops equity under $98,000, the maximum loss becomes the binding one, with less room than the daily figure would suggest. Bitfunded's max loss is static (fixed to the quota, per its help centre); BrightFunded's and Crypto Fund Trader's trail the high-water mark instead, which changes this arithmetic — ask if that's the account in question.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** size_trade · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **17.4 s**

> The stop is what turns a dollar risk target into a position size — without it, there's no way to know how many units correspond to the risk being taken.
> 
> **Formula:** `qty = risk / (|entry − stop| + fee_unit)`, where risk is the dollar amount troid is sizing for (a percent of equity, capped against the firm's remaining budget), and fee_unit is the round-trip fee added onto the stop distance.
> 
> **Why it works:** risk on a trade is fixed by the distance between entry and stop, times quantity. Flip that around and quantity is risk dollars divided by that distance. Two trades risking the same $500 can have very different position sizes depending on whether the stop is 0.3% away or 3% away — the tighter stop allows a much larger quantity for the same dollar risk.
> 
> **Worked example**, using a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step:
> 
> On a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step (daily loss limit 4% of the initial balance, help centre and Terms of Use 9(a), read 2026-09-23), a long at 100,000 with a 1% stop (99,000) risking $500:
>
