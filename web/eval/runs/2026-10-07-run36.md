## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **11.8 s**

> Two different ceilings, measured from different reference points, and they swap which one binds depending on the day's starting balance.
> 
> - **Daily loss limit**: a fixed dollar amount, set as a percentage of the account's **initial balance** (not the day's equity). On Bitfunded's 1-Step that's 4% of the starting $100,000 = $4,000. It resets with each new trading day: `daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)`.
> - **Maximum loss**: a static floor set a fixed percentage below the **starting quota**, and it never resets while the account is open. On the 1-Step that's 6% of $100,000, so the floor sits at $94,000 for the life of the account. Its budget is `dd_budget = equity − dd_floor`.
> 
> Why it matters: these two don't always move together. Early on, with the account near $100,000, the daily limit is the tighter constraint ($4,000 vs $6,000 of room). But after the account has already given back some ground on a prior day, the $94,000 floor can be closer than a fresh day's $4,000 allowance.
> 
> Worked example (Bitfunded 1-Step, $100,000 quota): the two swap exactly where day-start equity = quota × (1 − max% + daily%) = $98,000. Above $98,000 at the day's start, the daily limit binds (and binds all the way up through and above the $100,000 start too). Below $98,000 at the day's start, the max-loss floor binds instead, and the room left that day is under $4,000 — smaller than the $6,000 figure, which only applies on a day that starts at $100,000.
> 
> What it means: which ceiling is binding on any given day depends on that day's starting balance, not just on current equity — both floating and realized losses count against both ceilings, and reaching either one fails the account with no close required.
