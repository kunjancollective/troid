## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **14.0 s**

> Two different ceilings, and only one binds at a time.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** — the floor for a single trading day. It resets each day, and its size is a fixed amount taken from a fixed reference balance (not the current one). It caps how much can be lost from that reference point before the day is over.
> 
> **Maximum loss** — the floor for the whole account, from opening to close-out. It doesn't reset. Once equity crosses it, the account fails regardless of how the day started.
> 
> Formula for each, and which one binds:
> 
> ```
> daily_floor  = day_start_reference − quota × daily_pct
> daily_budget = equity − daily_floor
> 
> dd_floor     = quota × (1 − max_pct)          [static product]
>              = high_water_mark − quota × max_pct   [trailing product, until it locks]
> dd_budget    = equity − dd_floor
> 
> binding = whichever budget is smaller
> ```
> 
> Why it matters: the two floors sit at different distances from the current equity depending on where equity is relative to the quota. Below a crossover point, the max-loss floor is the tighter one and the "daily limit" figure a firm advertises is not actually what would stop you first.
> 
> Worked on troid's reference account — a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step, both loss types static, checked 2026-09-23/2026-09-21/2026-09-18:
> 
> - daily floor = $100,000 − 100,000×4% = **$96,000** → daily budget = $4,000
> - max-loss floor = 100,000×(1−6%) = **$94,000** → drawdown budget = $6,000
> - binding today: the **daily loss limit**, at $4,000 (the smaller of the two)
> - crossover equity = quota×(1 − max% + daily%) = **$98,000**: at any day-start balance below $98,000, the max-loss floor becomes the tighter one instead, and the advertised 4% daily figure is no longer the true limit that day.
> 
> For this account, at $100,000 equity, losing more than $4,000 today ends the day's budget before the $6,000 maximum-loss buffer is touched — the daily limit is what actually governs right now.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **16.7 s**

> The stop price sets the loss, and the loss is what determines how large a position can be.
> 
> **Formula:** `position size = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee per side × 2)`, and `risk = |entry − stop| × quantity` is the loss on the trade if the stop is hit.
> 
> **Why it works:** troid can't size a trade from the entry alone — a stop 0.3% away and a stop 3% away allow very different position sizes for the same dollar risk. The stop distance is the only thing standing between "risk 0.5% of the account" and an actual quantity to trade. Without it, there's no loss to size against, only a price.
> 
> **Worked example**, entry $77,872, stop $76,580 (a hypothetical, not the user's own), risking $500:
> 
> The stop distance is 1,292 (entry − stop), the fee per unit is 62.2976 (entry × 0.04% × 2, Bitfunded's per-side fee, SOURCED to its help centre, read 2026-09-18 and 2026-09-23), and dividing the $500 risk by the stop distance plus fee gives a quantity of 0.369195, a notional of $28,749.96.
> 
> **What it means:** without the stop, troid has a risk figure ($500) and nowhere to put it — quantity is undefined. The stop turns "how much am I willing to lose" into "how many units get me there." A tighter stop means a larger quantity for the same dollar risk; a wider stop means a smaller one. It's also why a stop given as a percent (say, 0.3%) is priced from entry and side rather than typed in as a price — the direction matters, since a long's stop sits below entry and a short's sits above.
