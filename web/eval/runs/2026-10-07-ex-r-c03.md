**Question:** What does R mean?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **24.0 s**

> R is the amount risked on a single trade — the loss if the stop is hit, used as a common unit so every trade's outcome, win or loss, can be measured on the same scale regardless of position size.
> 
> Formula: 1R = |entry − stop| × quantity
> 
> Why it works: a $500 loss on a $20,000 account and a $500 loss on a $100,000 account mean very different things in dollar terms, but both are "1R" if that's what was risked — so measuring results in R lets trades of different sizes be compared and averaged.
> 
> Worked example, for a long entered at 77,872 with a stop at 76,580 and a quantity of 0.3862: the stop distance is |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292, and 1R = 1,292 × 0.3862 = $498.97. A trade stopped out at that distance loses 1R, $498.97.
> 
> troid's desk also folds the trading fees into 1R — the entry fee on the entry notional and the exit fee on the exit notional, charged at the stop — so the real loss if this trade is stopped out runs a little above $498.97 once those are added.
> 
> For your own position: whatever quantity and stop distance you use, 1R is fixed by the formula above — it's the dollar loss at the stop, and every other outcome on that trade is naturally expressed as a multiple of it.
>
