**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **28.1 s**

> No — not while the margin fits inside the account's equity. Bitfunded's 1-Step caps leverage at 5×, so 10× isn't available there; the example below uses 2× and 5×.
> 
> **Answer:** the loss at the stop is the same, $500.00, at either leverage — leverage changes only the margin posted and how close liquidation sits, not the loss.
> 
> **Formula:**
> quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee × (entry + stop))
> notional = quantity × entry
> margin = notional ÷ leverage
> loss at the stop = quantity × (|entry − stop| + fee × (entry + stop)) = risk — *unless* that margin is bigger than the account's equity, in which case the size gets cut to equity × leverage ÷ entry, and the loss is *smaller*, not larger.
> 
> **Worked example** (entry 100, 1% stop, $500 risk, Bitfunded 1-Step's 0.04%-per-side fee, $100,000 equity):
> 
> At 2× leverage: quantity ≈ 463.134494, notional = $46,313.45, margin = $23,156.72, loss at the stop = $500.00.
> 
> At 5× leverage (the cap on this product, used in place of 10×): quantity ≈ 463.134494, notional = $46,313.45, margin = $9,262.69, loss at the stop = $500.00.
> 
> **What it means:** on this trade, at either leverage the margin posted fits comfortably inside $100,000 equity, so quantity, notional and the $500.00 loss at the stop are identical — only the margin tied up differs ($23,156.72 at 2× vs. $9,262.69 at 5×). Higher leverage only starts to matter for the size and the loss once the margin it would require exceeds the account's equity; then the position gets cut down to fit, and the loss shrinks rather than grows. Higher leverage also brings an isolated position's exchange liquidation closer to entry; under cross margin, which is troid's default assumption here (Bitfunded's margin-mode choice has no recorded source), leverage doesn't move the liquidation distance at all.
