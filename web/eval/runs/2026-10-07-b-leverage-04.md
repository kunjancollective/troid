## b-leverage — beginner

**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **27.0 s**

> No — leverage sets the margin posted, not the loss at the stop.
> 
> Formula: quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee × (entry + stop)); margin = (quantity × entry) ÷ leverage; loss at the stop = quantity × (|entry − stop| + fee × (entry + stop)) = risk. Leverage doesn't appear in the loss formula at all — it only divides into the margin.
> 
> Worked example: $100,000 equity, long entry 60,000, stop 59,400 (a 1% stop), risking $1,000, Bitfunded's 0.04%-per-side fee.
> 
> - At 2×: quantity 1.543782, notional $92,626.90, margin $46,313.45, loss at the stop = $1,000.00.
> - At 10×: same quantity 1.543782, same notional $92,626.90, margin $9,262.69, loss at the stop = $1,000.00.
> 
> The quantity and the loss are identical; only the margin posted changes (DERIVED). Bitfunded's 1-Step caps leverage at 1:5 (SOURCED — help centre, Challenge & Trader Stage, and Terms 9(a)), so 10× isn't available on that account at all; the 10× case above is shown generically, not against a firm's rule.
> 
> What this means for you: as long as the margin at your risk-based size fits inside your equity, raising leverage from 2× to 10× changes only how much margin is tied up, not what you lose if the stop is hit — the loss is set by the stop distance and the size, not the leverage. Leverage only starts changing the loss when the margin at the full risk-based size would exceed your equity: then the size gets cut to equity × leverage ÷ entry, and a lower leverage would cut it further, to a smaller loss.
