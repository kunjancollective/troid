**Question:** What does R mean?

**Tools:** trade_math, size_trade · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **20.1 s**

> R is the amount risked on one trade — the loss if the stop is hit, used as a unit so trades of different sizes can be compared on one scale.
> 
> Formula: 1R = |entry − stop| × position size.
> 
> Why it works: measuring every outcome as a multiple of the initial risk (−1R for a full stop-out, +2R for a gain twice the risk) puts trades with different entries, stops and sizes on the same scale, so expectancy and win-rate math can be done across them.
> 
> In practice: long BTC at 77,872 with a stop at 76,580 and a position of 0.3862 BTC, the stop sits 1,292 below entry, so 1R = 1,292 × 0.3862 = $498.97. troid's desk also counts the fees in the risk — the entry fee at entry, the exit fee at the stop — which on Bitfunded's 1-Step (0.04% a side) adds $23.86 here, bringing the desk's 1R to $522.83.
> 
> Sized against a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step account at 0.5% risk, that same entry and stop give a position of 0.369336 BTC, a risk (loss at the stop) of $500, and fees of $22.82.
