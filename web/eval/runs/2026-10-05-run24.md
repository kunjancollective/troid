**Question:** What does R mean?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **38.9 s**

> R is the amount risked on a single trade — the loss (or gain) that happens if the stop is hit, used as a unit to measure results on one scale.
> 
> Formula: `1R = |entry − stop| × quantity` (troid's desk adds both fees: `+ fee × (entry + stop) × quantity`, the exit fee charged at the stop); a trade's result in R = `result ÷ 1R`.
> 
> Why it works: a dollar loss means something different on different account sizes and different trade sizes. Expressing every outcome as a multiple of what was risked puts them on one scale.
> 
> In practice: long BTC at entry 77,872, stop 76,580, size 0.3862 BTC. Stop distance = |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292, so the raw 1R = 1,292 × 0.3862 = 498.97. Bitfunded's fee is 0.04% per side (help centre, Criteria to be Success), charged at entry and again at the stop, adding 23.86, so troid's desk counts 1R with fees as 522.83. A result of 1,000 on this trade works out to 1,000 ÷ 522.83 = 1.913R.
> 
> What it means for you: a trade's result divided by its own risk turns any dollar outcome into a figure that compares directly across trades of different sizes — and against a firm's loss ceilings, which are measured the same way.

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **12.8 s**

> troid doesn't recommend; it prices what you bring. Here is what Kelly implies from the numbers given.
> 
> Formula: `f* = p − (1 − p) ÷ b`, where p is win rate and b is average win ÷ average loss.
> 
> Why it works: Kelly's fraction maximises long-run growth rate — but only if p and b are known exactly, which they never are in live trading; they're estimates from a sample.
> 
> In practice, with p = 0.45 and b = 2: f* = 0.45 − 0.55 ÷ 2 = 17.5% of the account per trade (full Kelly). Half Kelly, the fraction many traders use to cut variance, is 17.5% ÷ 2 = 8.75%.
> 
> What this means on an account troid prices: Bitfunded's 1-Step caps the maximum loss at 6% of the starting balance and the daily loss at 4% (help centre, Challenge & Trader Stage, and Terms 9(a), read 2026-09-23). Even half Kelly, at 8.75%, is 1.46 times the maximum loss and 2.19 times the daily limit — one losing trade at that size breaches both ceilings outright. On a product with these ceilings, the firm's loss limit binds long before Kelly's fraction would.

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **11.5 s**

> 
> Formula: gain needed = d ÷ (1 − d), where d is the drawdown as a fraction.
> 
> Why it works: a loss is measured on the starting balance, but the recovery gain is measured on the smaller balance left after it — so the percentage needed back is always larger than the percentage lost.
