> R is the size of one unit of risk on a trade — the amount lost if the stop is hit.
> 
> **Formula:** `1R = |entry − stop| × position size`
> 
> **Why it matters:** measuring results in R lets trades of different sizes and instruments be compared on one common scale — "+2R" means the same thing whether the position was small or large.
> 
> **In practice** (worked with a round example, since none were given): long BTC at 77,872 with a stop at 76,580, position size 0.3862 BTC. The stop is 1,292 below entry, so:
>

## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.3 s**

> Every funded account has two separate loss ceilings, and they answer different questions.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** — how much the account can lose in one trading day before it fails.
> 
> ```
> daily_limit  = quota × daily_loss%
> daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)
> ```
> 
> It resets each day. Under Bitfunded it is measured off the *initial* balance (fixed dollar amount, not a moving target), and today's net profit or loss on the account shifts how much room is left before the next reset. A profitable morning widens today's daily budget; a losing one narrows it, floor included.
> 
> **Maximum loss (the "floor")** — how much the account can ever be down from where it started (or, for trailing products, from its peak) before it fails, no matter how many days that takes to happen.
> 
> ```
> dd_floor  = quota × (1 − max_loss%)          [static, e.g. Bitfunded]
> dd_floor  = high_water_mark × (1 − max_loss%) [trailing, e.g. BrightFunded, CFT]
> dd_budget = equity − dd_floor
> ```
> 
> **Why the distinction matters:** the two budgets aren't always the same size, and whichever is *smaller* is the one that actually stops the trade first — that's the "binding" ceiling. troid's desk computes `effective_budget = min(daily_budget, dd_budget)` and sizes against that, not against the advertised headline number.
> 
> Two structural differences worth naming, both SOURCED to Bitfunded's own documents (help centre, "Criteria to be Success," read 2026-09-18):
> - **Static vs trailing:** a static floor (Bitfunded) is measured from the account's starting quota — profit permanently widens the buffer. A trailing floor (BrightFunded, Crypto Fund Trader) follows the high-water mark up, so after a run-up and a pullback the trader can have *less* room than at the start.
> - **Crossover:** on a fresh day the two ceilings swap which one binds at a specific equity level. Below that equity, the max-loss floor governs even though the daily limit is the one usually advertised.
> 
> If you give troid a specific firm, product, quota and equity, it can compute both budgets and the crossover point for that account exactly.
> 
> Sources, each with the date troid read it:
> - drawdown type (static) — Bitfunded help centre — Criteria to be Success (the mechanics: a static floor measured from the account quota), read 2026-09-18
> - BrightFunded drawdown (trailing on equity) — BrightFunded help centre — 'How does my daily permitted loss work' and 'BrightFunded 1-Step', Scenario 3, equity high-water mark, read 2026-09-22
> - Crypto Fund Trader drawdown (trailing on balance) — source not yet recorded

## b-leverage — beginner

**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** none · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **16.2 s**

> No — the loss at the stop is the same either way; leverage changes margin and liquidation distance, not the loss.
> 
> **Formula:** risk = |entry − stop| × quantity. Leverage does not appear in this formula at all.
> 
> **Why it works:** the loss on a stopped-out trade is determined purely by the distance between entry and stop, multiplied by position size (quantity). Leverage only determines how much margin you post to control that position size — `margin = notional / leverage` — and where the exchange's own liquidation price sits, not what you lose when your own stop order fires.
> 
> **In practice:** say you hold a position where entry − stop distance produces a $500 loss at a given quantity.
