**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **17.2 s**

> The stop price sets the size of the loss, and the loss is what troid sizes against.
> 
> Formula: risk = |entry − stop| × quantity, so quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee per unit). The dollar risk troid works from — a percent of equity, capped against whichever loss ceiling binds — is fixed first; the stop distance is what turns that dollar figure into a quantity.
> 
> Why it works: two trades risking the same dollar amount can have very different position sizes if their stops sit at different distances from entry — a tight stop allows a larger quantity for the same risk, a wide stop a smaller one. Without the stop, "risk 0.5% of equity" has no quantity attached to it.
> 
> In practice, take entry 77,872 and a stop 1,292 away (troid does not invent this figure — it is here only to work the formula):
> 
> With $500 risked and a 1,292 stop distance, the tool derives a quantity of 0.369195 (fees included), notional $28,749.96, and margin $5,749.99 at 5× leverage. Change the stop to, say, 2,584 away with the same $500 risk, and quantity roughly halves — the stop is what converts a dollar risk into a size.
> 
> For you: without the stop, troid has a dollar risk figure and nothing to divide it by — it can't produce a quantity, a notional, or a margin. That's also why a stop given as a percent goes through troid as stop_pct rather than a price troid or the user computes by hand: the arithmetic runs through the same formula either way.
