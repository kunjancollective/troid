## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **16.7 s**

> The stop is what turns a dollar risk into a position size — without it there's no distance to divide the risk by.
> 
> Formula: `qty = risk / (|entry − stop| + fee_unit)`, where risk is the dollar amount being risked on the trade and fee_unit is the round-trip fee added to the risk distance.
> 
> Why it works: the position size that makes the loss equal to the intended risk depends entirely on how far price has to move to hit the stop. A tighter stop means a smaller distance, so more units are needed to risk the same dollar amount; a wider stop means fewer units. Two traders risking the same dollars but using different stops end up with very different position sizes and very different margin requirements.
> 
> In practice, on Bitfunded's 1-Step:
> 
> Risking $500 with entry 77,872 and stop 76,580 (a 1,292 distance, plus 62.30 of round-trip fee per unit at Bitfunded's 0.04% per side) gives a quantity of 0.3692, a notional of $28,749.96, and margin of $5,749.99 at 5× leverage.
> 
> For you: the same $500 risk on a stop twice as far away would produce roughly half the quantity and half the margin — the stop is what fixes that ratio. Without it troid has a dollar amount and nowhere to put it. This is also why size_trade and check_budget always ask for the stop (or a stop_pct): it's the one input that connects risk to quantity, and it's yours to set, not troid's.
