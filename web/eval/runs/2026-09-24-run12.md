**Tools:** trade_math, firm_rules · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **24.8 s**

> R is the amount risked on one trade — the loss if the stop is hit, used as a single unit to measure every trade on the same scale.
> 
> **Formula:** `1R = |entry − stop| × quantity` (troid's desk adds the round-trip fee: `+ entry × fee × 2 × quantity`, since that's real money at risk too). A trade's result in R is then `result ÷ 1R`.
> 
> **Why it matters:** trades come in different sizes and different stop distances. Measuring the outcome in R multiples lets you compare a tight scalp and a wide swing trade on one scale — "+2R" means the same thing regardless of the dollar size.
> 
> **Worked example:** long at entry 77,872, stop at 76,580, quantity 0.3862.
> 
> - stop distance = |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292
> - 1R = 1,292 × 0.3862 = $498.97
> - round-trip fee (Bitfunded 1-Step, 0.04% per side) = 77,872 × 0.04% × 2 × 0.3862 = $24.06
> - 1R with fees, as troid's desk counts it = $498.97 + $24.06 = $523.03
> 
> So a trade that closes $1,046.06 in profit is +2R; one stopped out is −1R (or a bit worse than −1R once the fee is folded in, since the desk counts fees inside the risk).
> 
> **What it means for you:** if this were sized on a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step account, a risk of $523.03 is about 0.52% of the account. ## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **12.4 s**

> Two different ceilings, each measured from a different point, and each resets on a different clock.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** — a fixed amount, sized as a percent of the account's *initial* balance, that resets each trading day. For Bitfunded's 1-Step: `daily_limit = quota × 4%`. On a $100,000 account that's a fixed $4,000 per day, and today's floor sits at `day_start_equity − remaining_daily_budget`. It is not a percent of today's balance — it's always 4% of the original $100,000, and it resets on the day boundary.
> 
> **Maximum loss** — measured over the life of the account, not per day. On Bitfunded's 1-Step it's static, from the quota: `dd_floor = quota × (1 − 6%)`, i.e. $94,000 on a $100,000 account. It never resets, and once equity touches that floor the account is done. On a trailing-drawdown product (BrightFunded's 1-Step, Crypto Fund Trader's 1-Phase) the floor instead follows the account's high-water mark up as it makes profit, until it locks at the initial balance after a stated gain.
> 
> Both ceilings count **floating** losses on open positions, not just closed trades — on Bitfunded, an open position that reaches either one fails the account with no close required (help centre, Criteria to be Success, read 2026-09-24).
> 
> What this means in practice: the two limits bind at different equity levels, and one crossover point exists where they swap which one is tighter. Below that crossover, the maximum-loss floor is the one that actually constrains sizing even though the daily limit is the number usually advertised.
