>

>

> daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)

>

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **18.7 s**

> The stop price sets the position size — without it there's no way to convert a dollar risk into a quantity.
> 
> Formula: `quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + entry × fee_per_side × 2)`
> 
> Why it works: the dollar amount troid is willing to lose on the trade (the risk) is fixed. What one unit of the instrument loses if the stop is hit is `|entry − stop|`, plus the round-trip fee. > - stop distance = |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292

> - quantity = 500 ÷ (1,292 + 62.30) = 0.3692 units

> - margin at 5× leverage = $28,749.96 ÷ 5 = $5,749.99

## b-leverage — beginner

**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** explain_rule · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **15.6 s**

> No — the loss at the stop is the same either way.
> 
> **Formula:** `risk = |entry − stop| × quantity`. Leverage does not appear in that equation at all.
> 
> **Why:** the loss on a trade is set by the price distance between entry and stop, multiplied by how many units you hold — not by how much margin you posted to hold them. Leverage only changes the margin required to open the position and, correspondingly, how far price can move against you before the exchange forces you out (liquidation).
> 
> **Worked example:** suppose entry is 50,000, stop is 49,500 (a distance of 500), and quantity is 0.1. `risk = 500 × 0.1 = 50` — this loss is identical at 2x or 10x leverage. What changes is the margin: at 2x, margin = notional ÷ 2 = (50,000 × 0.1) ÷ 2 = 2,500; at 10x, margin = 5,000 ÷ 10 = 500. Less margin is tied up at 10x, but the dollar loss when the stop is hit is 50 in both cases.
> 
> **What it means:** raising leverage from 2x to 10x frees up margin and moves any exchange liquidation price closer to entry — under isolated margin, roughly `entry × (1 − 1/leverage)`, so about 10% away at 10x versus 50% away at 2x. It does not increase the loss the stop itself produces. The exception is if higher leverage tempts a larger position size for the same margin outlay — that's a sizing choice, not a leverage effect, and it's the one thing that would actually increase the loss.
