## b-leverage — beginner

**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **20.3 s**

> No — not while the margin fits in the account's equity.
> 
> Formula: quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee × (entry + stop)); notional = quantity × entry; margin = notional ÷ leverage; loss at the stop = quantity × (|entry − stop| + fee × (entry + stop)) = risk. Leverage appears only in the margin line, not in the quantity or the loss line — so the loss at the stop is the same number whatever the leverage, as long as the margin that size needs is no more than the account's equity.
> 
> Worked example (Bitfunded 1-Step reference account, $100,000 equity, long at 50,000 with a stop at 49,500, $500 risked):
> 
> - quantity = 0.926269, notional = $46,313.45, fee per unit $39.80 (0.04% per side, Bitfunded help centre — Criteria to be Success), loss at the stop = $500, regardless of leverage.
> - At 2× leverage: margin = notional ÷ 2 = $23,156.72 — fits inside $100,000 equity.
> - At 10×: not available here — Bitfunded caps the 1-Step at 1:5 (help centre, Challenge & Trader Stage, One Step table; Terms of Use 9(a)), so this account can't take that trade at 10× at all.
> 
> What this means: on a Bitfunded 1-Step, going from 2× to the account's maximum 5× leaves the $500 loss at the stop unchanged — only the margin posted drops, from $23,156.72 at 2× to a smaller figure at 5×, and the exchange-liquidation distance on an isolated position moves closer to entry. What actually sets the size of the loss is the risk chosen and the stop distance, not the leverage. If a leverage is high enough that the margin it needs exceeds equity, troid's desk cuts the position size to fit the margin — and then the loss at the stop is smaller, not larger.
