## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **14.1 s**

> Two separate ceilings, and they bind at different times.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** — a floor measured from where the account stood at the start of the trading day (or, on Bitfunded, a fixed amount computed from the initial balance — help centre FAQ). It resets every day. Formula:
> 
> ```
> daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)
> ```
> 
> Today's net P&L moves this budget; yesterday's result doesn't carry into it, but a loss still open when the day rolls over counts at full size against the new day (Bitfunded help centre, Criteria to be Success — floating losses are in the calculation, and an open position that reaches either limit fails the account, no close required).
> 
> **Maximum loss** — the account's overall floor, measured from either the quota (static) or a high-water mark (trailing), depending on the product:
> 
> ```
> static:   dd_floor = quota × (1 − max%)
> trailing: dd_floor = high_water_mark − quota × max%   (locks at the quota after the profit threshold)
> dd_budget = equity − dd_floor
> ```
> 
> Bitfunded's 1-Step maximum loss is static, so a profit permanently widens this floor's buffer. BrightFunded's 1-Step and Crypto Fund Trader's 1-Phase trail: the floor follows the account up as it makes new highs, until it locks. Crypto Fund Trader's 2-Phase is static.
> 
> **Why it matters:** whichever budget is smaller at a given moment is the one that actually governs the next trade — troid calls this the binding budget. Early in a challenge, or after a loss, the daily limit is usually tighter and binds; well above the account's starting balance, the maximum loss usually binds instead, at a point called the crossover equity. Sizing against the wrong one is a common way accounts fail even though the trader believed the "smaller" limit was the one in play.
> 
> For a concrete number on this — the crossover equity, or which one binds on a specific account and balance — that takes an equity, quota and product; troid can work it through check_budget if you give those.
> 
> Sources, each with the date troid read it:

> - BrightFunded drawdown (trailing on equity) — BrightFunded help centre — 'How does my daily permitted loss work' and 'BrightFunded 1-Step', Scenario 3, equity high-water mark, read 2026-09-22

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **25.7 s**

> The stop price sets the entire size — without it, no position size can be computed at all.
> 
> **Formula:** `qty = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee_unit)`, where risk is the dollar amount troid allows on the trade and fee_unit is the round-trip fee cost per unit.
> 
> **Why it works:** the dollar loss on a trade is fixed by how far price has to move against the position before it's closed — `|entry − stop| × qty` — not by the entry price, the account size, or the leverage chosen. Given a dollar risk (say, 0.5% of equity), the only way to convert that into a quantity is to divide by the distance to the stop. A tighter stop means a larger position for the same dollar risk; a wider stop means a smaller one. Without a stop distance, "risk" has no units to divide by, so no quantity can be produced.
> 
> **Worked example**, on troid's reference account, a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step:
>
