## b-leverage — beginner

**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **20.5 s**

> No — the loss at the stop stays the same whether leverage is 10x or 2x, as long as the margin needed fits inside the account's equity.
> 
> **Formula:** quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee per unit); notional = quantity × entry; margin = notional ÷ leverage; loss at the stop = quantity × (|entry − stop| + fee per unit) = risk. Leverage appears only in the margin line, not in quantity or in the loss — unless the margin that quantity needs is above the account's equity, in which case the size is cut to quantity = equity × leverage ÷ entry, and then a lower leverage does mean a smaller size and a smaller loss.
> 
> Worked at risk = $500, entry 77,872, stop 76,580, on a $100,000 account:
> 
> - Stop distance = |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292
> - Quantity = 500 ÷ 1,292 = 0.386997, the same at either leverage
> - Notional = 0.386997 × 77,872 = 30,136.22, also the same at either leverage
> - At 2x: margin = 30,136.22 ÷ 2 = 15,068.11
> - At 10x: margin = 30,136.22 ÷ 10 = 3,013.62
> - Loss at the stop = 0.386997 × 1,292 = $500 at either leverage
> 
> Both margins sit under the account's $100,000 equity, so neither leverage triggers the cut-to-fit rule. The loss stays $500 at 2x and at 10x; only the margin posted differs — $15,068.11 versus $3,013.62 — and, under isolated margin, where liquidation sits relative to entry (closer at 10x). Under cross margin, troid's default model, leverage doesn't move the liquidation distance at all.
