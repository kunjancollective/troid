## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **17.9 s**

> They are two separate floors on the account, and the smaller distance to either one governs at any moment.
> 
> **Formula:**
> ```
> daily_floor = day_start − quota × daily_loss%      (resets each trading day)
> daily_budget = equity − daily_floor
> 
> dd_floor = quota × (1 − max_loss%)                 (static — doesn't move with equity)
> dd_budget = equity − dd_floor
> 
> effective_budget = min(daily_budget, dd_budget)    ← whichever is smaller "binds"
> ```
> 
> The daily limit resets to a fresh floor at the start of every trading day, measured from that day's starting balance (or, on Bitfunded, from the initial balance — a fixed dollar amount, not a moving one). The maximum loss floor doesn't reset; on a static-drawdown product it sits a fixed distance below the account's original quota for the life of the challenge.
> 
> **Why it matters:** early in a challenge, with equity near the quota, the daily limit is usually the tighter one — a single bad day can breach it before the max loss even comes close. As the account gets closer to its overall floor (through losses accumulated over several days), the max loss becomes the binding constraint even though the daily limit looks unbreached that day.
> 
> **Worked example**, troid's reference account — Bitfunded's $100,000 1-Step, equity and day-start both at $100,000:
> 
> - daily floor = $100,000 − 4% × $100,000 = $96,000 → daily budget = $4,000
> - dd floor = $100,000 × (1 − 6%) = $94,000 → dd budget = $6,000
> - binding = min($4,000, $6,000) = **daily loss limit, $4,000**
> 
> **What it means for this account:** at $100,000 equity, $4,000 of room is available before the daily limit trips, even though $6,000 of distance remains to the maximum-loss floor. The two swap which one binds at $98,000 equity (quota × (1 − max% + daily%)) — below that starting balance, the max-loss floor becomes tighter than the advertised daily limit.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **18.1 s**

> Because the stop distance is what turns a risk percentage into an actual position size — without it, "risk 0.5%" is just a number with no quantity attached.
> 
> Formula:
> `qty = risk / (|entry − stop| + fee_unit)`, where `risk = risk% × equity` and `fee_unit = entry × fee_per_side × 2`
> 
> Why it works: the dollar amount troid is willing to lose (`risk`) has to be divided by how far price moves against the position before it's stopped out, in price terms — plus the round-trip fee, which is a loss regardless of where the stop sits. A tighter stop means a larger position for the same dollar risk; a wider stop means a smaller one. Without the stop distance, there's no way to convert a percentage of equity into units of the asset.
> 
> Worked example, using Bitfunded's 1-Step fee (0.04% per side):
> 
> Risking $500 with entry 77,872 and stop 76,580 (a 1,292-point stop), the quantity comes to 0.369195, on notional of $28,749.96, needing $5,749.99 in margin at 5× leverage.
> 
> What it means: change the stop and the same $500 risk buys a different quantity — a tighter stop (say 500 points) would let more units be bought for the same dollar risk, a wider one fewer. That's why troid asks for the stop before it can size anything: it's not an optional detail, it's the divisor.

## b-leverage — beginner

**Question:** If I use 10x leverage instead of 2x, do I lose more when my stop is hit?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.7 s**

> No — the loss at the stop is the same either way; leverage changes the margin posted and where liquidation sits, not the risk.
> 
> **Formula:** risk = |entry − stop| × quantity. Leverage does not appear in this formula at all.
> 
> **Why it works:** the loss on a trade is determined purely by how far price moves against you (entry to stop) times how many units you hold. Leverage only determines how much margin you post to control that same position size — it's a financing choice, not a risk choice. Two traders holding identical quantity with identical stops lose identical dollars, whether one used 2x and the other 10x.
> 
> **Worked example:** consider a long entry at 50,000 with a stop at 49,000 (a $1,000 stop distance) and a quantity of 0.1 units.
>

>

> - Notional value = quantity × entry = 0.1 × 50,000 = $5,000
> - At 2x, margin posted = $5,000 ÷ 2 = $2,500
> - At 10x, margin posted = $5,000 ÷ 10 = $500
>
