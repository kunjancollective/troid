## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.9 s**

> Both are loss ceilings an account must not touch, but they reset on different clocks and bind at different times.
> 
> **The daily loss limit** resets every trading day and measures loss against the day's starting point. On Bitfunded's 1-Step it is 4% of the account's **initial balance** (a fixed dollar amount, not a shrinking percentage of equity) — source: Bitfunded FAQ, read 2026-09-21. It only cares about what the account lost *since the last reset*, at 16:00–16:10 UTC (Bitfunded help centre, Criteria to be Success, read 2026-09-23/24).
> 
> **The maximum loss** (sometimes called max drawdown) is cumulative over the whole account's life, not reset daily. On Bitfunded's 1-Step it is 6%, static — measured from the account's quota (the starting balance), not a trailing high-water mark, so profit permanently widens the buffer (Bitfunded help centre, Criteria to be Success, read 2026-09-18). Other firms' products trail a high-water mark instead (e.g. BrightFunded's 1-Step, CFT's 1-Phase) — this is a per-product rule, not a per-firm one.
> 
> **Working, on troid's reference account** ($100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step):
> 
> On a fresh $100,000 account at equity $100,000:
> 
> - **daily floor** = day start − quota × 4% = $100,000 − $4,000 = **$96,000**, giving a daily budget of $4,000.
> - **max-loss floor** = quota × (1 − 6%) = **$94,000**, giving a drawdown budget of $6,000.
> - the **binding** one here is the daily loss limit ($4,000 of room), since it's tighter at this equity.
> 
> **Crossover.** The two swap at equity = quota × (1 − 6% + 4%) = **$98,000** on this account — $2,000 below the starting balance. Above $98,000 the daily limit is the binding ceiling; below it, the max loss takes over and the daily limit effectively becomes irrelevant (it's wider than what's left before the max loss triggers).
> 
> What this means: the daily limit is the one that bites early, on a good or flat account. The max loss is the one that catches an account that has already slipped — once equity is down enough, losing the day's allowance doesn't matter because the account fails on cumulative loss first. Both count floating losses on open positions, not just closed ones, and Bitfunded auto-fails on either without requiring the position to be closed (Bitfunded help centre, Criteria to be Success, read 2026-09-24).

> ask troid does not run simulations, including Monte Carlo ones, with any inputs — none are generated live here.
> 
> What troid can do instead is two things:
> 
> 1. **The closed-form expectancy and break-even for your numbers**, computed through the tools, not simulated.
> 2. **Point to troid's published Monte Carlo results**, which are MODELLED (stated assumptions, not your exact inputs) and labelled as such — they're about naive fixed-fractional risk against a firm's ceiling (e.g. 68% of simulated years hitting ruin at 1% risk under one set of assumptions), not a simulation of your specific win-rate/payoff pair.
> 
> Working the deterministic part now:
> 
> **One line: with this win rate and payoff, expectancy is +0.21R per trade, and over 100 trades the mean outcome is +21R — but that's a mean over many runs, not what any single run of 100 trades will do.**
> 
> **Formula:** `E = p × W − (1 − p) × L`, where p is win rate, W is average win in R, L is average loss in R.
> 
> **Why it works:** this is the expected value per trade if every trade is drawn from the same distribution — a long-run average, not a prediction for any particular sequence.
> 
> **Worked example (your numbers):**
> - p = 0.55, W = 1.2R, L = 1R
> - E = 0.55 × 1.2 − 0.45 × 1 = 0.66 − 0.45 = **0.21R per trade**
> - Break-even win rate at this payoff ratio (1.2) = 1 ÷ (1.2 + 1) = **45.45%** — your 55% is above it.
> - Expected total over 100 trades = 100 × 0.21 = **21R**
> 
> **What's missing for an actual Monte Carlo:** the mean (21R) says nothing about the spread — the actual distribution of outcomes across 100 trades, the probability of a deep drawdown along the way, or the chance of breaching a firm's loss ceiling mid-sequence before reaching the mean. That requires a simulation across many random orderings of wins and losses, and troid doesn't run new ones here.
