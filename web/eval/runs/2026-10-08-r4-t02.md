## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.2 s**

> The daily loss limit and the maximum loss are two separate ceilings, and only one of them binds at a time.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** resets every day and is measured from a fixed point: on Bitfunded's 1-Step it's a fixed amount — 4% of the *initial* balance, never recalculated off equity — and the floor it sets is the day's starting balance minus that amount.
> 
> **Maximum loss** is measured over the life of the account, from the quota (on Bitfunded's 1-Step it's static: 6% of the initial balance, so profit permanently widens the buffer rather than a floor that trails the account up).
> 
> Formula for each:
> `daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)` — today's net P&L offsets the fixed daily limit
> `dd_budget = equity − dd_floor`, where `dd_floor = quota × (1 − max_loss%)` for a static product like Bitfunded's 1-Step
> 
> Worked example, on troid's reference account, a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step: the daily floor is $100,000 − 4% × $100,000 = $96,000, giving a daily budget of $4,000. The max-loss floor is $100,000 × (1 − 6%) = $94,000, giving a drawdown budget of $6,000. The binding ceiling is min($4,000, $6,000) = $4,000, the daily loss limit.
> 
> Which one binds turns on the day's start, not on equity alone. The crossover — where the two are equal — is quota × (1 − max% + daily%) = $98,000. Below $98,000 at the day's start, the max-loss floor binds and is the real limit; between $98,000 and the $100,000 start, and above it, the daily limit binds. The daily floor itself is always the day's start minus the fixed daily amount, never a static number carved from the quota — so the $4,000/$6,000 split above only holds on a day that starts at $100,000.
> 
> What this means for the reference account: at the start, the daily limit is the tighter, binding ceiling ($4,000 of room), even though the maximum loss is advertised as the larger percentage (6% vs 4%).

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **21.7 s**

> The stop sets the size because it defines how much is lost per unit if the trade goes wrong — without it, no dollar risk can be computed at all.
> 
> Formula: quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee per unit), where fee per unit = (entry + stop) × fee per side, since the exit fee is charged at the stop. Then notional = quantity × entry, margin = notional ÷ leverage, and loss at the stop = quantity × (|entry − stop| + fee per unit), which equals the risk — unless the margin needed is more than the equity available, in which case the quantity is cut to equity × leverage ÷ entry and the loss at the stop is less than the risk.
> 
> Worked example (entry 77,872, stop 76,580, risk $500, Bitfunded 1-Step fee 0.04% per side, 5× leverage, $100,000 equity):
> 
> - stop distance = |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292
> - fee per unit = (77,872 + 76,580) × 0.04% = 61.78
> - quantity = 500 ÷ (1,292 + 61.78) = 0.369336
> - notional = 0.369336 × 77,872 = 28,760.93
> - margin = 28,760.93 ÷ 5 = 5,752.19 (below the $100,000 equity, so no cut applies)
> - loss at the stop = 0.369336 × (1,292 + 61.78) = 500 — exactly the intended risk
> 
> What it means: the stop distance is the denominator in the sizing formula. Move the stop and the same $500 risk buys a different quantity — a tighter stop would increase the quantity for the same dollar loss, a wider one would reduce it. Without a stop there is no distance to divide the risk by, so no quantity, no notional, and no margin can be computed — the stop is what turns a dollar risk into a position size in the first place.
