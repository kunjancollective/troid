## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **18.3 s**

> The daily loss limit resets each day; the maximum loss (drawdown) does not — it tracks the account's loss from its starting point for the life of the challenge, and whichever is tighter on a given day is the one that actually binds.
> 
> Formula, on Bitfunded's 1-Step:
> daily_floor = day_start − quota × daily% ; daily_budget = equity − daily_floor
> dd_floor = quota × (1 − max%) ; dd_budget = equity − dd_floor
> binding = min(daily_budget, dd_budget)
> 
> Why it works: the daily limit is a fixed amount measured from the day's opening balance and resets at every rollover, so it only ever constrains today's losses. The maximum loss is measured from the account's quota (or, for a trailing product, from its high-water mark) and accumulates across every day the account has traded, so it is the ceiling that governs the whole challenge, not just today.
> 
> Worked example, troid's reference account — a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step at the day's start, 4% daily and 6% maximum, daily measured from the initial balance, static drawdown:
> 
> - daily floor = 100,000 − 100,000×4% = 96,000 → daily budget = 100,000 − 96,000 = $4,000
> - max-loss floor = 100,000×(1−6%) = 94,000 → drawdown budget = 100,000 − 94,000 = $6,000
> - binding = min(4,000, 6,000) = the daily loss limit, $4,000
> 
> What it means: on a day that starts at $100,000, the account can lose $4,000 before the daily limit stops it, even though $6,000 of room sits against the maximum loss — the daily limit is the one that actually ends the day. The crossover where the two swap is at a day-start equity of $98,000 (quota × (1 − max% + daily%)): below that, the maximum-loss floor binds instead, because there's less than $4,000 of room left between equity and the (now-nearer) floor. Both budgets count floating losses on open positions, not just closed ones.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **15.5 s**

> The stop sets the loss per unit, and the loss per unit sets how many units a given risk buys — so without it troid has no position size to compute.
> 
> Formula: qty = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee_unit), where fee_unit = fee_per_side × (entry + stop) is the per-unit cost of opening at entry and closing at the stop.
>
