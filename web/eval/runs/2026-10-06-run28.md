> R is the amount risked on one trade — the loss if the stop is hit, used as a single unit to measure every trade's result on the same scale.
> 
> Formula: `1R = |entry − stop| × quantity` (troid's desk adds both fees into the risk: `+ fee × (entry + stop) × quantity`, the exit fee charged at the stop price).
> 
> Why it works: dollar P&L depends on position size, which varies trade to trade. Measuring in R — multiples of what was risked — lets trades of different sizes be compared on the same scale: a win or loss is stated as a multiple of what was put at risk, not a raw dollar figure.

> - Fees in and out = 0.04% × (77,872 + 76,580) × 0.3862 = $23.86

>

## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **21.5 s**

> Every funded account carries two loss ceilings, and they're measured differently — and they bind at different times.
> 
> **Daily loss limit** — a fixed dollar amount, set from the account's *initial* balance, that resets each trading day. On Bitfunded's 1-Step, on a $100,000 account, that's `quota × 4% = $4,000` (daily budget, on a day starting at $100,000). The floor it sets moves each day with that day's starting balance: `daily floor = day start − quota × 4%`, which on this account is $96,000.
> 
> **Maximum loss** — a static floor measured once, from the starting quota, that never moves. On the same account: `max-loss floor = quota × (1 − 6%) = $94,000`, giving a drawdown budget of `equity − max-loss floor = $6,000`.
> 
> **Why it matters:** at any moment the account is governed by whichever budget is smaller — `effective budget = min(daily budget, drawdown budget)` — and which one that is depends only on the day's starting balance, not on equity alone. On this account, with equity and day-start both at $100,000, the daily loss limit is the binding one, at $4,000 of room.
> 
> The two swap at `crossover equity = quota × (1 − max% + daily%) = $98,000` — just $2,000 below the $100,000 start. Below $98,000 at the day's start, the max-loss floor binds and the daily limit isn't the real constraint that day; between $98,000 and the $100,000 start, and above it, the daily limit binds. A day that starts less than $2,000 below the start is still bound by the daily limit, however much was lost or given back before it; only a day that starts below $98,000 is bound by the floor.
> 
> Both ceilings count floating losses on open positions, not just closed ones — Bitfunded fails an account that *reaches* either limit, with no close required.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **21.9 s**

> The stop price is what sets the size of the trade: troid can't size a position without it.
> 
> **Formula:** quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee per unit)
> 
> **Why it works:** the dollar amount lost if the stop is hit equals quantity × distance to the stop (plus fees). Risk is fixed by the trader's choice (a percent of equity); distance to the stop is fixed by where the stop sits. Quantity is the only variable left to solve for — so the stop isn't optional information, it's the divisor in the one equation that produces a position size.
