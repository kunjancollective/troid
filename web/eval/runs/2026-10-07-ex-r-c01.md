**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.6 s**

> R is the amount risked on one trade — the loss if the stop is hit, used as a unit so trades of different sizes can be compared on one scale.
> 
> Formula: 1R = |entry − stop| × quantity (and troid's desk adds both fees to the risk: + fee × (entry + stop) × quantity, the exit fee charged at the stop).
> 
> Worked example: a long at entry 77,872, stop 76,580, size 0.3862. The stop distance is 1,292, so 1R = 1,292 × 0.3862 = $498.97. troid's desk also counts the entry and exit fees in the risk — at Bitfunded's 1-Step rate of 0.04% per side, that adds $23.86, making 1R = $522.83 (DERIVED).
> 
> What it means: once a trade's 1R is fixed, every other trade's outcome can be stated as a multiple of it — a close that gains twice the stop distance is +2R, one stopped out is −1R — which is why expectancy, Kelly and ruin calculations are all expressed in R rather than in dollars.
