**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **25.7 s**

> R is the amount you risk on one trade — the loss if your stop is hit — used as a unit to measure results on one scale regardless of position size.
> 
> **Formula:** `1R = |entry − stop| × position size` (troid's desk adds both fees to this: + fee × (entry + stop) × quantity, the exit fee charged at the stop)
> 
> **Why it works:** a loss of a given dollar amount on a small account and the same dollar loss on a large account are very different in relative terms, but if both trades risked the same dollar figure, they are both "1R" trades. Measuring every outcome in R lets you compare trades of different sizes, and compute statistics like expectancy or win rate on one consistent scale.
> 
> **Worked example:** long BTC at entry 77,872 with a stop at 76,580, position size 0.3862 BTC:
> 
> - Stop distance = |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292
> - 1R = 1,292 × 0.3862 = $498.97
> - Bitfunded's trading fee (0.04% per side, charged at entry and again at the stop) adds fee × (entry + stop) × quantity = $23.86
> - 1R with fees = $498.97 + $23.86 = $522.83 (DERIVED, using Bitfunded's 1-Step fee, per its help centre — Criteria to be Success)
> 
> A close with a result of $998 on this trade is $998 ÷ $522.83 = 1.909R; a stop-out is −1R before fees, or a little worse than −1R once the fees are counted, since fees are a real cost on a losing trade too.
> 
> **What it means for you:** measuring trades in R, rather than in dollars, lets you track your win rate and average win/loss on one consistent scale no matter what size each trade was.

## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.8 s**

> The daily loss limit resets every trading day; the maximum loss (drawdown) limit doesn't — it tracks the account from inception (or from its high, if trailing). Whichever gives the smaller remaining budget at a given moment is the one that actually binds.
> 
> **Formula:**
> 
> ```
> daily_floor  = day_start − quota × daily_loss%
> daily_budget = equity − daily_floor
> 
> dd_floor     = quota × (1 − max_loss%)        (static drawdown)
> dd_budget    = equity − dd_floor
> 
> binding      = min(daily_budget, dd_budget)
> ```
> 
> **Worked example** — Bitfunded's $100,000 1-Step, day starting at $100,000, equity now $96,500:
> 
> - daily floor = day start − quota × 4% = $96,000
> - daily budget = equity − daily floor = $96,500 − $96,000 = $500
> - max-loss floor = quota × (1 − 6%) = $94,000
> - drawdown budget = equity − max-loss floor = $96,500 − $94,000 = $2,500
> - binding = min($500, $2,500) = the daily loss limit, $500
> 
> For this account, the daily limit binds — $500 of room, not $2,500 — because it's the smaller of the two: the next $500 of loss ends the day's trading regardless of how much further the maximum-loss floor sits below.
>
