## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **13.2 s**

> The daily loss limit resets every day; the maximum loss doesn't — it's measured against the whole account from the start (or, on a trailing product, from the high-water mark).
> 
> Formula, on a fresh day:
> `daily_floor = day_start − quota × daily%` → `daily_budget = equity − daily_floor`
> `dd_floor = quota × (1 − max%)` (static) or `high_water_mark − quota × max%` (trailing, locks at the quota)
> `dd_budget = equity − dd_floor`
> The binding ceiling is `min(daily_budget, dd_budget)`.
> 
> Why it matters: each is a different floor under the same equity, and only one actually constrains a trade at a time — sizing against the wrong one can make a trader think they have more room than they do.
> 
> Worked on troid's reference account, a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step (daily 4%, max 6%, static, both read 2026-09-23/2026-09-18), day-start = equity = $100,000:
> - daily floor = 100,000 − 100,000×4% = $96,000 → daily budget = $4,000
> - max-loss floor = 100,000×(1−6%) = $94,000 → max-loss budget = $6,000
> - binding = min($4,000, $6,000) = **daily loss limit, $4,000**
> 
> The crossover where the two swap is `quota × (1 − max% + daily%)` = $98,000: below a day-start of $98,000 the max-loss floor binds instead, and the floor is the day-start less the daily amount, never a static number read off the quota. Both budgets count floating losses on open positions, and reaching either fails the account — no close required.
> 
> For this account: at a day-start of $100,000, only $4,000 of loss is available before the account fails, not the $6,000 that the maximum-loss rule alone would suggest.

## b-stop — beginner

**Question:** Why does troid need my stop price to size a trade?

**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **13.8 s**

> The stop sets the position size: it's what turns a dollar risk into a quantity.
> 
> Formula: quantity = risk ÷ (|entry − stop| + fee_unit), where fee_unit = fee × (entry + stop) (the exit fee is charged at the stop, below entry on a long, above it on a short). The dollar risk is fixed (a percent of equity or balance), so the wider the stop, the smaller the position has to be to keep that dollar loss the same; a tighter stop allows a larger position for the same dollar loss.
> 
> Worked example, through troid's desk: entry 77,872, a 1% risk on a $100,000 account ($1,000), stop 1.5% away.
