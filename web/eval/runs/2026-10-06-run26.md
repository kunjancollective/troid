**Tools:** trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **19.6 s**

> R is the amount risked on one trade — the loss if the stop is hit — used as the unit to measure every trade's result on the same scale.
> 
> Formula: `1R = |entry − stop| × position size`. A trade's result in R is `result ÷ 1R`. troid's desk also folds in both trading fees (entry and exit), since the exit fee is charged at the stop: `1R with fees = 1R + fee × (entry + stop) × quantity`.
> 
> Why it works: a dollar loss means nothing by itself — it could be a tiny slip on a huge position or the whole risk on a small one. Measuring in R (risk units) makes trades of different sizes comparable: "−1R" always means "lost what was risked," whatever the dollar size.
> 
> Worked example: long at entry 77,872, stop at 76,580, position size 0.3862. The stop distance is |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292. So 1R = 1,292 × 0.3862 = $498.97. A trade closed at 2× that distance above entry is +2R; a trade stopped out is −1R, or about −$522.83 once fees are counted.
> 
> What it means for you: stating a result as "+2R" or "−1R" already accounts for the actual dollar risk, including fees — so comparing it against another trade of a different size, in R, is a comparison on the same scale. (DERIVED from the numbers given and Bitfunded's published fee rule.)

## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **22.2 s**

> A funded account has two loss ceilings that bind at different times, and the one that currently binds sets how big a position can be.
> 
> **Formulas (DERIVED, on Bitfunded's 1-Step, SOURCED rules below):**
> 
> ```
> daily floor   = day start − quota × daily%
> daily budget  = equity − daily floor
> 
> max-loss floor = quota × (1 − max%)
> drawdown budget = equity − max-loss floor
> 
> binding = min(daily budget, drawdown budget)
> ```
> 
> Why it works: the daily limit resets every day and is measured against that day's starting balance, so a profitable day gains room and a losing day loses it, but the fixed amount it's calculated from never changes size. The maximum loss sits under the whole account from day one, measured from the quota, and (being static on Bitfunded) widens permanently as the account books profit. The two ceilings answer different questions — "how much can today cost" versus "how much can the account ever lose" — so whichever is numerically closer binds.
> 
> **Worked example**, troid's reference account, a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step at a day start of $100,000: the daily floor is day start − quota × 4% = $96,000, giving a daily budget of equity − daily floor = $4,000. The max-loss floor is quota × (1 − 6%) = $94,000, giving a drawdown budget of equity − max-loss floor = $6,000. The binding ceiling is min(daily budget, drawdown budget) = the daily loss limit, at $4,000.
> 
> The crossover — the day-start balance at which the two swap — is max-loss floor + quota × 4% = quota × (1 − 6% + 4%) = $98,000, exactly $2,000 below the starting balance:
> 
> - Below $98,000 at the day's start, the maximum-loss floor binds — the daily limit is not the real constraint that day.
> - Between $98,000 and $100,000, and above $100,000, the daily limit binds.
> - A day starting less than $2,000 below the start is still bound by the daily limit, not "a small amount".
> 
> Both of Bitfunded's ceilings count floating losses: an open position that reaches either one fails the account, with no close needed.
> 
> What this means: on troid's reference account, a trader can't size against the advertised 4% daily number alone without checking the day's starting balance against $98,000 first — below that, the real ceiling is the maximum loss.
