## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** none · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **10.8 s**

> Two different ceilings, and they bind at different times.
> 
> **The daily loss limit** resets on a schedule and measures loss against a fixed reference point (which depends on the firm). Hit it and the account fails for that day's activity, but the ceiling itself refreshes.
> 
> **The maximum loss** (also called max drawdown) never resets during the challenge. It's measured against either a static floor (a fixed distance below the starting balance) or a trailing floor (a fixed distance below the account's high-water mark, which rises as equity rises). Hit it once, at any point, and the account fails permanently.
> 
> Formulas troid uses:
> 
> ```
> daily_limit  = quota × daily_loss%
> daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)     [today's P&L offsets it]
> 
> dd_floor (static)   = quota × (1 − max_loss%)
> dd_floor (trailing) = high_water_mark − quota × max_loss%   [stops trailing at the quota]
> dd_budget = equity − dd_floor
> ```
> 
> Why it matters: early in a challenge, close to the starting balance, the daily limit is usually the tighter (binding) constraint. But as losses accumulate, the maximum-loss floor gets closer and *it* becomes binding — often well before the daily limit would trip on its own. There's a crossover equity level where the two swap, and below it, the daily limit's advertised size is irrelevant because the max loss floor is what actually stops the trader first.
> 
> troid doesn't fill in a specific firm's percentages from memory here — those numbers, and which drawdown type (static vs. trailing) a given firm uses, come from that firm's own rules. If there's a specific firm and product, troid can pull the daily%, max%, and the crossover equity for it.
> 
> Not financial advice. Verify with the firm before acting.

>
