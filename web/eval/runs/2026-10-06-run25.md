**Tools:** trade_math, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **22.3 s**

> R is the amount risked on one trade — the loss if the stop is hit.
> 
> Formula: `1R = |entry − stop| × quantity`. On troid's desk, fees are counted into the risk too, since they're paid whether the trade wins or loses: a unit's fee is `fee × (entry + stop)` — the entry fee charged at entry, the exit fee charged at the stop — so `1R with fees = 1R + fees`. A result's size in R is then `result ÷ 1R with fees`.
> 
> Why it matters: measuring every result in R puts trades of different sizes and instruments on one scale, so "a 2R winner" means the same thing regardless of position size.
> 
> In practice: a long on BTC, entry 77,872, stop 76,580, quantity 0.3862.
> - stop distance = |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292

## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **21.6 s**

> The daily loss limit resets every trading day; the maximum loss is a single floor for the life of the account.
> 
> Formula: daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start); dd_budget = equity − dd_floor, where dd_floor = quota × (1 − max%) for a static-drawdown product like Bitfunded's 1-Step. The binding one is whichever budget is smaller. They swap where day_start equity = quota × (1 − max% + daily%).
> 
> Why it works: the daily limit measures only what happens since the day's reset, so a profitable day widens how much more can be lost that day, while the maximum loss measures from the account's starting quota (or, on a trailing-drawdown product, from the high-water mark) and doesn't reset.
> 
> Worked example, on troid's reference account — a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step (daily 4%, max 6% static, both SOURCED from the help centre's Challenge & Trader Stage table and Terms 9(a), read 2026-09-23; the daily basis is the initial balance, FAQ, read 2026-09-21), with equity and day-start both at $100,000: the daily floor is day_start − quota × 4% = $96,000, giving a daily budget of equity − daily floor = $4,000. The maximum-loss floor is quota × (1 − 6%) = $94,000, giving a drawdown budget of equity − that floor = $6,000. The binding one is the smaller, the daily loss limit at $4,000. The crossover equity is $98,000 — the max-loss floor plus quota × 4%, or quota × (1 − 6% + 4%) — $2,000 below the $100,000 start, half of one day's loss limit. Below $98,000 at the day's start, the maximum-loss floor binds and the 4% daily figure isn't the live constraint that day; between $98,000 and $100,000, and above the start, the daily limit binds instead. A day that starts less than $2,000 below the start is still bound by the daily limit, never "any slip."
> 
> What it means: which ceiling actually constrains sizing on a given day depends on that day's starting balance, not just on current equity — both must be checked, and the smaller budget governs. Both of Bitfunded's ceilings also count floating losses on open positions, not only closed ones, and failing either one fails the account with no close required (help centre, Criteria to be Success, read 2026-09-24).

> Why it works: Bitfunded's 1-Step has two loss ceilings. The maximum loss is a static floor set from the starting quota: quota × (1 − 6%) = $94,000. The daily limit is a fixed dollar amount taken from the initial balance — 4% of $100,000 = $4,000 — subtracted from the day's starting balance, giving a daily floor on a fresh day of $100,000 − $4,000 = $96,000. Since the daily amount is fixed while the max-loss floor is also fixed, the two floors cross at one equity point: below it, the max-loss floor sits closer to equity and binds; above it, the daily floor sits closer and binds. On a fresh $100,000 day, the daily floor ($96,000) sits above the max-loss floor ($94,000), so the daily limit is the binding one, with $4,000 of room against it, against $6,000 of room under the max-loss floor.
> 
> What it means: on a day that starts below $98,000, the 6% maximum-loss floor is the binding constraint and the advertised 4% daily limit is not what actually stops the trader that day. On a day that starts at $98,000 or above — including above the $100,000 starting balance — the daily limit binds. $98,000 is only $2,000 below the starting balance, half of one day's $4,000 loss limit, so an account doesn't have to be deep in drawdown before the tighter floor takes over. Both ceilings count floating losses on open positions, not just closed ones, and Bitfunded auto-fails the account the moment either one is reached — no close required.
