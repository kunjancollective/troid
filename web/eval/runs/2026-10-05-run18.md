## ex-r — beginner (a worked example in TROID-CHARACTER.md)

**Question:** What does R mean?

**Tools:** trade_math, check_budget, firm_rules, trade_math · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **34.8 s**

> R is the amount risked on one trade — the loss if the stop is hit.
> 
> **Formula:** `1R = |entry − stop| × position size` (troid's desk also folds in both fees: the one paid to open at entry and the one paid to close at the stop, since both are part of what's actually lost).
> 
> **Why it works:** measuring every trade's result in multiples of R puts trades of different sizes and different stop distances on one common scale, so "+2R" means the same thing whether the position was large or small.
> 
> **In practice:** long BTC at entry 77,872, stop at 76,580, position size 0.3862 BTC.
> - Stop distance: |77,872 − 76,580| = 1,292
> - 1R (price distance only): 1,292 × 0.3862 = $498.97
> - Bitfunded's 1-Step charges 0.04% per side on the notional, at entry and again at the stop: 0.04% × (77,872 + 76,580) × 0.3862 = $23.86 of fees
> - 1R including fees: $498.97 + $23.86 = **$522.83**
> 
> A close stopped out here loses about $522.83 — that's −1R. A close that moves twice as far in the trade's favor as the stop is away would be +2R.
> 
> **What it means:** on a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step with equity still at $100,000, the daily loss limit is the binding ceiling, with $4,000 of room (daily basis: the initial balance). Against a loss of $522.83 per trade, 4,000 ÷ 522.83 ≈ 7.65, so the 8th loss of that size is the one that reaches the limit — 7 losses of that size leave equity above it, with about $340.19 of room remaining after those 7.

## ex-kelly — quantitative (a worked example in TROID-CHARACTER.md)

**Question:** Should I size with Kelly? I win 45% of my trades and my average win is twice my average loss.

**Tools:** trade_math, firm_rules · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **17.3 s**

> troid doesn't recommend; it prices what you bring. What it can show is what Kelly implies, and what that implies for a funded account.
> 
> **Formula:** `f* = p − (1 − p) / b`, where p is the win rate and b is the average win divided by the average loss (the payoff ratio).
> 
> **Why it works:** full Kelly maximises long-run growth rate — but only if p and b are known exactly, which they never are in live trading. Estimates drift, and Kelly is unforgiving of overestimation.
> 
> **In practice:** p = 0.45, b = 2 → f* = 0.45 − 0.55/2 = 0.175, or 17.5% of the account risked per trade. Half-Kelly, a common reduction for estimation error, is 17.5% ÷ 2 = 8.75%.
> 
> **What it means for you:** on a Bitfunded 1-Step, the daily loss limit is 4% and the maximum loss is 6%, static. A single full-Kelly-sized loss is 17.5% ÷ 6% = 2.92 times the account's entire maximum-loss allowance, and 17.5% ÷ 4% = 4.38 times the daily limit. Even half-Kelly at 8.75% is 1.46× the maximum loss and 2.19× the daily limit — one loss at that size breaches both ceilings outright. The firm's ceiling binds long before Kelly would, which is why troid sizes trades against the remaining budget (daily and maximum loss), not against a Kelly fraction.

> 
> Formula: gain needed = d ÷ (1 − d), where d is the drawdown as a fraction.
> 
> Why it works: a loss is measured against the starting balance, but the recovery gain is measured against the smaller balance left after the loss — so the percentage needed to get back is always larger than the percentage lost.
> 
> In practice: d = 0.20 → gain needed = 0.20 ÷ (1 − 0.20) = 0.25, or 25%.
> 
> For you: a 20% drawdown is already past every maximum loss troid has read on the products it covers — the largest is 10%, on Bitfunded's 2-Step Stage 1 and Crypto Fund Trader's 2-Phase. ## p-crossover — prop-rule

**Question:** What is the crossover on a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step, and why does it matter?

**Tools:** explain_rule, trade_math, trade_math, check_budget · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **20.7 s**

> **The crossover on a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step is $98,000.**
> 
> **Formula:** `crossover = quota × (1 − max% + daily%)`, equivalently `max-loss floor + quota × daily%`
> 
> Bitfunded's 1-Step carries a 4% daily loss limit and a 6% maximum loss, both static floors measured from the account's quota (the starting balance). The daily limit is a fixed amount from the initial balance, not from the day's starting equity (Bitfunded FAQ).
> 
> **Why it works:** the two loss ceilings move independently. The maximum-loss floor is fixed at `quota × (1 − 6%) = 94,000` and never moves. The daily limit resets each day as a fixed amount (`quota × 4% = 4,000`) of room below the day's starting balance. Below a certain day-start balance, the max-loss floor is actually closer than the daily limit, so it's the one that binds, and the advertised daily cushion overstates how much room there really is that day. The crossover is where the two meet.
> 
> **Worked example:** on a $100,000 quota, `100,000 × (1 − 0.06 + 0.04) = $98,000`. At day-start equity of $100,000, the daily floor sits at $96,000 (daily budget $4,000), the max-loss floor at $94,000 (drawdown budget $6,000) — the daily limit is the binding one here, since $4,000 < $6,000. At $500 risked a trade, that $4,000 daily budget leaves 7 losses before the one that reaches the limit.
> 
> **What it means:** if the account's day-start balance is above $98,000, the daily limit is the binding constraint for that day. If it starts at or below $98,000 — only $2,000 below the original balance, half of one day's $4,000 daily allowance — the maximum-loss floor binds instead, and sizing against the daily limit alone would understate the real risk of failing the account. Both ceilings count floating (open, unrealized) losses, and a position that reaches either one fails the account with no close required (Bitfunded help centre, Criteria to be Success).
