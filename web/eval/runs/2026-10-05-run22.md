## b-limits — beginner

**Question:** What's the difference between the daily loss limit and the maximum loss?

**Tools:** explain_rule · **model:** claude-sonnet-5 · **9.6 s**

> The daily loss limit and the maximum loss are two separate ceilings on the same account, and only one of them actually binds on any given day.
> 
> **The daily loss limit** is a fixed dollar amount measured from the account's initial balance (not the day's starting equity). On Bitfunded's 1-Step it is 4% of the quota. It resets each day: `daily_budget = daily_limit + (equity − day_start)` — today's running P&L offsets it.
> 
> **The maximum loss** is a floor measured from the starting quota (static, on Bitfunded's 1-Step): `dd_floor = quota × (1 − max%)`, 6% on the same product. It never resets — it's the account's lifetime loss ceiling, not a daily one.
> 
> **Why one binds at a time:** on a $100,000 Bitfunded 1-Step, the two swap at day-start equity of $98,000 — `quota × (1 − max% + daily%) = 100,000 × (1 − 0.06 + 0.04) = 98,000`. Below $98,000 at the day's start, the maximum-loss floor is the binding constraint (the daily limit's 4% would let you lose further than the floor allows). Between $98,000 and $100,000, and above it, the daily limit binds instead.
> 
> Both ceilings count floating (open, unrealized) losses, not just closed ones — an open position that reaches either one fails the account with no close required, per Bitfunded's help centre.
> 
> What it means: sizing against only the daily limit is only safe when day-start equity is above $98,000 on this product; below that, the maximum loss is the tighter constraint and the daily figure is the wrong one to size against.
>
