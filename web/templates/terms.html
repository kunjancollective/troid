2. Description of the service

troid is a free, informational tool for traders taking prop-firm challenges. It provides:

  • troid's desk: a position-size calculator. It works out, for a trade you enter, how much room a firm's loss limits leave and which limit binds first.
  • troid's compare: the rules of the firms troid lists, side by side. A rule troid has not filled in shows as pending. Not every rule shown has yet been confirmed from the firm's own documents; section 4 explains.
  • troid's ledger: a public record of a simulated strategy, scheduled to update every four hours. Every trade it shows from before 21 September 2026 was backfilled on that date from a backtest on api.binance.us data for 8 January to 21 September 2026. The strategy's parameters were chosen on a Binance.com sample of 8 January to 14 September 2026, so all but the last week of that backtest is in-sample. The page says how many trades were logged live. No real money is traded and no real orders are placed.
  • troid's research: a dashboard, a tearsheet and a methodology page about that simulated strategy and about how troid tests its claims.
  • ask troid: an AI assistant, described in section 10.

troid does not place, modify or close orders. It does not connect to any brokerage, exchange or prop-firm account. No troid page asks for a login, password, API key or other credential. troid publishes no trading signals and recommends no trade, strategy or firm. ask troid cannot place orders either, and it is instructed not to recommend a trade or a firm and never to ask for a credential. Section 10 says what to do if it breaks those instructions. troid has no user accounts.

troid's ledger shows a simulated trade only after the trade has closed. troid does not publish the direction, price or stop of an open position, but the strategy's code and data are public, so anyone who runs it can reproduce its positions. It is not a signal service, and it is not published for you to copy. Some firms' contracts prohibit using a third-party or marketed strategy, or trade ideas taken from others, on a challenge. Copying the ledger onto a challenge account may breach those rules.

troid sets no cookies and runs no analytics. troid's pages are served by a hosting provider, which receives your IP address with every request. Most of troid's pages load fonts from Google Fonts, and troid's ledger loads a chart library from cdn.jsdelivr.net. Those providers receive your IP address when they serve the file. When you follow a troid affiliate link, the firm's site may set its own cookie to record the referral. That cookie is the firm's, under the firm's terms.

Whether to buy a challenge, whether to trade, and how much to risk are your decisions alone. If you want advice, ask a qualified professional who knows your circumstances.

4. Accuracy of third-party rule data

The firm rules troid shows are third-party information. troid reads them from each firm's own published documents, such as its terms of use, help centre and product pages, and lists those documents in troid's rule data. Beside each rule a result uses, troid's desk and troid's compare show the document, the section and the date troid read it. Where troid shows a value but has not yet recorded its source, the page says the source is not yet recorded. Such a value may have come from a firm's marketing pages or from a third-party directory, and has not been checked against the firm's contract. A rule troid has no value for shows as pending.

troid marks a firm verified only after reading the firm's terms and its help centre against each other. Not every firm troid lists is verified, and even for a verified firm some rules do not yet have a recorded source. troid's compare shows, for each firm, how many rules are filled and how many have a recorded source, and the date troid last reviewed which firms it lists.

Firms change their rules, prices and products without notice. Sometimes a firm's own pages disagree with its own contract. troid does not warrant that any rule it shows is complete, is current, or matches the rules the firm applies to your account today. troid can be slow to catch a change, can read a document differently from the firm, and can make mistakes.

When troid finds an error, it corrects it and records the correction in the corrections table in troid's methodology. If you find one, send it to hello@troid.ai.

5. Your obligation to verify

Before you buy a challenge, and before you place any trade on one, check every rule and number that matters to you against the firm's current documents and your account dashboard. Where troid and the firm disagree, the firm decides. For your account's own numbers, such as balance and equity, the firm's dashboard is the reference. For the rules, the firm's contract governs. Where the firm's own pages or dashboard disagree with its contract, ask the firm in writing which it applies before you rely on either.

Check, at least:

  • the daily and maximum loss limits, and how each one is measured;
  • when the firm's trading day resets;
  • fees, leverage and margin rules;
  • holding limits, position limits and minimum trading days;
  • prohibited practices;
  • payout and refund conditions;
  • whether the firm serves residents of your country.

troid's calculations are only as good as the inputs you enter. Make sure your balance, equity, day-start balance and every other input match your account at the moment you use them.

6. Hypothetical results

troid's ledger, troid's research and troid's backtests show the results of a simulated strategy. No real money was traded and no real orders were placed. Every trade on troid's ledger up to 21 September 2026 was backfilled on that date from a backtest, not recorded as it happened. The ledger page shows how many have been logged live since.

These results are based on simulated or hypothetical performance results that have certain inherent limitations. Unlike the results shown in an actual performance record, these results do not represent actual trading. Also, because these trades have not actually been executed, these results may have under-or over-compensated for the impact, if any, of certain market factors, such as lack of liquidity. Simulated or hypothetical trading programs in general are also subject to the fact that they are designed with the benefit of hindsight. No representation is being made that any account will or is likely to achieve profits or losses similar to these being shown.

troid's own strategy shows no statistical edge. Tested on data from 1 January 2021 to 7 January 2026, which it was not tuned on, it measured +0.008R per trade on BTC (504 trades) and +0.008R on ETH (498 trades). R is the amount risked on one trade. For both, the 95% confidence interval includes zero, so the result is inside noise. Until a strategy passes that test, treat every number troid publishes about its strategy as noise. Past results, simulated or real, do not predict future results.

7. No guarantee of passing any challenge

troid does not guarantee, and does not predict, that you will pass any challenge, reach a funded account, or receive any payout.

Correct position sizing does not create an edge. A correctly sized trade can still breach a loss limit through a price gap, slippage or a run of losses. Nothing troid computes stops a firm from applying its rules, its discretion or its penalties to your account.

8. What a prop challenge is

A prop-firm challenge is an evaluation you pay for. In general, the account you trade is a demo, or simulated, account. Your orders are not sent to a real market, and the balance is not money you own or can withdraw. A payout, if the firm makes one, is paid under the firm's own terms, which can let it reduce or refuse the payout.

In general, a prop firm is not a broker and holds no client funds for you. The fee you pay is the price of the evaluation, not a deposit. Broker regulation, client-money rules and investor-compensation schemes generally do not apply. Apart from the general consumer-protection law where you live, your protections are the firm's contract and nothing else. Read the contract before you pay: who you are contracting with, which country's law applies, what ends an account, and when a payout can be refused.

Some firms also offer a route that trades a real exchange account, sometimes in your own name. There, the exchange's own terms and risks apply as well. Check which kind of account you are buying.

9. Affiliate disclosure

troid is an independent affiliate of the firms it compares. When you buy a challenge through a troid link or code, the firm may pay troid a commission under its affiliate terms. troid adds nothing to the price you pay.

The commission is paid on the purchase, not on your result, so troid is paid whether you pass or fail. Some firms also pay troid a smaller commission when you buy again, including after a failed challenge. That is a conflict of interest, and you should weigh everything troid publishes with it in mind.

Commissions do not affect the calculations, the rules troid shows, or the order in which firms appear, which is alphabetical. troid does not rank or score firms itself, and does not recommend one. Where a page repeats a third-party figure about a firm, such as a review count or an outside ranking, it names the source. troid is not an employee, agent or representative of any firm. No firm owns or operates troid, and no firm is responsible for its content.

The current firms, and the criterion for choosing them, are on troid's compare.

Not every rule in that data has been confirmed from the firm's own documents (see section 4). Some of ask troid's rule explanations are written separately and can fall out of step with troid's compare.

ask troid can be wrong. An AI model can misread a question, misstate a rule, answer from outside that data, or misreport what a tool returned. The firm's contract and your account dashboard are the reference, as section 5 explains. Check anything that matters with the firm before you act on it.

ask troid is instructed not to recommend a trade or a firm. It works out the numbers for a trade you describe. It is instructed to answer questions such as “should I”, “which firm is best for me”, “will I pass” or “what should I trade” with one fixed reply: troid doesn't recommend; it prices what you bring. If you tell ask troid a number was wrong, it is instructed to ask for or reconstruct your inputs, to reproduce the calculation step by step with each rule and the date troid read it (or a note that the rule's source is not yet recorded), and to point you to the firm's dashboard and to hello@troid.ai. It is instructed not to argue, and not to say whose fault a loss was.

If ask troid recommends a trade, a firm or a strategy, says whether you will pass, or asks for a credential, it has made a mistake. Do not act on it, and tell troid at hello@troid.ai.

What happens to what you type:

  • Each session has a session ID: a random number your browser creates when the session starts, shown under the message box. So that no one can rewrite ask troid's side of a conversation or move it to another session, each reply carries a signature over the conversation and its session ID, which the page sends back with the next message. ask troid keeps no memory between sessions; reloading the page starts a new session.
  • troid keeps each conversation for 30 days after its last message, under its session ID, and then deletes it automatically. It keeps the time of each message, the page's language, your messages and ask troid's replies, each tool ask troid used with its inputs and results, the rule sources and read dates cited, and the AI model used. It does not keep your IP address, your browser's user agent, your name or any account.
  • troid keeps conversations to answer disputes about what ask troid said, to correct errors, to check that ask troid keeps to the guardrails described in this section, and to understand which questions come up most, so it can improve its pages and answers.
  • For that last purpose, once a week troid counts that week's messages by topic (for example “position size” or “the daily reset”), by the firms and ask troid tools they involve, and by the page's language. The counts are made by fixed rules, not by reading or summarising any one conversation. They hold no text from any conversation, no quotation and no session ID. troid keeps them after the conversations themselves are deleted and does not publish them. Deleting a conversation removes it, but not counts already made from it, which cannot be traced back to it.
  • Only the Operator can read a kept conversation. No page or interface on this site returns one. troid never sells conversations and never uses them for marketing or to train any AI model. They are held for the Operator by a third-party database provider.
  • To delete a conversation before the 30 days are up, use “delete this conversation” on the ask troid page while the session is open, which deletes it at once, or write to hello@troid.ai with the session ID.
  • To produce each answer, troid sends the conversation so far to the company that provides the AI model. That company handles it under its own terms and may keep it for a limited period.
  • For each message ask troid answers or fails to answer, troid's server log also records one line: whether it was answered or failed; how many tool calls it used; which model was called; whether the conversation was kept; whether the reply was the abuse warning, the fixed reply given when the AI model declines to answer, or the end of the session; and, if the AI provider returned an error, its numeric status code. The log line does not record the text of your messages or your IP address. A message turned away before any call to the AI model (ask troid switched off or busy, over the per-address limit, too long, malformed or unverifiable) is neither logged nor kept.
  • ask troid limits each IP address to about 20 messages an hour (for IPv6, each /64 block of addresses). To do that, troid holds the address in the server's working memory until that server instance restarts. It is never written to troid's log or kept with a conversation.

Do not enter personal information, account numbers, passwords, API keys or any other credential. ask troid does not need them and is instructed never to ask for a credential.
